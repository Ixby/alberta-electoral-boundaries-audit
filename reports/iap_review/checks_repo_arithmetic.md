# Checks: arithmetic and 2023 results (repository sources)

Checked 2026-11-03 against the panel's App. 7 (PDF pp.211–212), the Commission final report's majority list (`evidence/commission_reports/abebc_2026_rpt_final.pdf`, PDF pp.45–56) and `data/reference/alberta_2023_results.csv`. Text-layer files are `evidence/legislature/iap_report_2026_hires_text/pNNN.txt` (PDF page numbers).

| ID | Claim | Verdict | What the check found |
|---|---|---|---|
| M16 | 25% of 53,722 is 13,430; band 40,292 to 67,152 (p.102) | Holds | Exact: 13,430.5, band 40,291.5 to 67,152.5. The minority's 67,153 rounds the same figure up. |
| M9 | "Lethbridge already has population to spare" (p.74) | Partly holds | Lethbridge-East 57,463 + Lethbridge-West 54,235 = 111,698 on the Commission's divisions (includes a small slice of Lethbridge County; inference). Two divisions at the average take 107,444, so it is 4,254 (4.0%) over the average. It is well inside what two divisions may hold under the 25% rule (134,304) and 9,178 short of three divisions' floor (120,876). "To spare" is true only against the exact average. |
| M10 | 2023 vote and seat shares; disproportionality indices (pp.103–104) | Cannot be checked as an index claim | The panel names Loosemore-Hanby, Gallagher and Rae but prints no index value. Its shares: UCP "roughly 52%" of votes and 49 seats "57%"; NDP "44%", 38 seats, "almost 44%". Recomputed: UCP 52.64% of votes, 56.32% of seats; NDP 44.06% and 43.68%. The UCP seat share rounds to 56%, not 57%. |
| N8 | Bearspaw and Springbank add "about an additional 350 persons per Calgary electoral division" (¶110–112) | Holds | Calgary's 28 districts average 56,725.6 on the panel's map against 56,379.1 on the Commission's: +346.5 per district (9,702 more people in all). The minority gives no computation in ¶110–112; the figure matches its Table 5. The difference is the whole-city change, mostly the added outside areas (inference). |
| N9 | Table 5: the changes "increase population disparities. In small ways" (¶147–151) | Holds as arithmetic; the framing needs a caveat | Every Table 5 figure reproduces. The percentages use different averages (54,929 for 89 seats, 53,722 for 91). Calgary's raw mean rose only 347 (+0.6%); about 2.3 of its 2.95-point rise comes from moving to 91 seats (inference). |
| N10 | Calgary and Lethbridge hold Alberta's most competitive divisions disproportionately (¶56) | Holds | 2023, UCP–NDP margin within 5 points: 14 districts, 13 of them in Calgary (12) or Lethbridge (1). Within 10 points: 14 of 18. Those cities hold 28 of 87 districts (32%). No Edmonton district is within 10 points. 2023 data only. |

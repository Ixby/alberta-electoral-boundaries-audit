Short summary

The explorer shows three maps of Alberta's ridings: the commission's minority map, its majority map, and the 2019 map, which is in use today. It does not show the panel's 91-riding map, the one before the Legislature. The audit's provisional results for that map are on the main page.

Five structural tests look only at the lines, with no election results. They were written down on April 24 and 25, after trial runs on approximate boundaries but before the official files arrived. Four of the five compare the minority map with the majority map, so they describe how the two differ. They are not a pass or a fail for the majority map. The minority map differs from the majority map on four of five comparisons. Separate tests use votes and compare each map with 1,010,000 maps a computer drew without seeing a vote.

Below, nine boundaries the audit flagged are explained, then every riding is listed. This page describes what the measurements show. It does not reach a legal conclusion about any map.

Flagged boundaries

The audit flagged nine boundaries. Some look odd but follow real geography. Others stand out on the audit's own measurements, though none meets its formal packing or cracking test. Each is explained below, with a section of the technical report where one applies. Counts of public submissions come from a full read of all 1,688 public records: 1,339 written submissions and 349 hearing presentations. AI agents did the reading under the author's direction. The counts are of records, not people, and a campaign letter counts each time it was sent.

Banff — the town carved out of the park

Banff sits inside a federal national park, as a small municipal island. The minority groups the townsite with Canmore — the next town down the Bow Valley — in “Canmore-Kananaskis,” and leaves the uninhabited park as the riding that carries Banff's name. The boundary hugs the town because it's the only populated spot for miles. It looks like a mistake, but it follows real geography.

North of Banff — “Rocky Mountain House–Banff Park”

“Rocky Mountain House–Banff Park” is one of the commission minority's proposed ridings. Much of it is national park where few people live; the town of Banff is in the riding to the south. The minority used the Act's remote-riding exception, section 15(2), which lets up to four ridings have far fewer people than average. The commission's majority called this “a bad faith effort” in the final report. The audit's check undercuts that charge: the riding qualifies for the exception even without the park. In a full read of the public submissions, 13 records ask for the park to join the Rocky Mountain House riding, as the minority drew it, and 2 ask for the majority's Bow Valley riding. For the riding's core, Clearwater County, Rocky Mountain House and Sundre together, 36 ask for what the minority drew and none for what only the majority drew.

Airdrie — split four ways

Airdrie's growth and its commute to Calgary (about 76% of out-commuters) could have anchored a single new Airdrie-area seat. The commission minority instead splits the city across four ridings. Its population does not require that. In a full read of the public submissions, 1 record asks for something like the minority's split, and 10 ask for what the majority drew. Cracking means splitting a community so thinly that it can't decide any of its seats. On the audit's cracking test, this split stops short. Outside Airdrie East, which holds more than half the city, Airdrie's residents are outnumbered, but the test needs them outnumbered in every piece. The fairness tests that use votes cannot see a split like this. The audit measures what it does, not why it was drawn. (Technical report, section 5.3.2.)

Calgary-Nolan Hill–Cochrane

Cochrane's commute to Calgary could have justified pairing it with the city. But the 2021 journey-to-work data spreads that commute across the whole city (about a third of workers; half work within Cochrane), not toward Nolan Hill. The commission minority's narrow corridor to the Nolan Hill ward reaches further than the data. It is in one of two ridings the commission's majority said “look strange”. In a full read of the public submissions, no record asks for Cochrane to be joined to northwest Calgary, and 7 ask for what the majority drew. The audit's cracking test does not flag it: Cochrane stays whole and makes up about two-thirds of the riding, and at about 34,000 people it is too small for a seat of its own. (Technical report, section 5.3.2.)

Olds–Three Hills–Didsbury — the Airdrie reach

A rural riding that keeps Olds, Didsbury and Three Hills together, along with Crossfield, Irricana and Beiseker, has public support. In a full read of the submissions, 29 records ask for that riding as the commission minority drew it, and 6 for the majority's version. It could have stayed within those communities. The minority's version instead reaches south into the northern edge of Airdrie, which the population does not require. No record mentions that reach. It is part of how the four-way Airdrie split works: a thinly settled strip of the city, about 350 people, goes into a large rural riding. By the audit's own cracking test, the Airdrie split is not cracking. (Technical report, section 5.3.2.)

Chestermere — split between two ridings

Chestermere's heavy commute to Calgary (86% of out-commuters) could have supported keeping the city whole and close to Calgary. The commission minority instead puts a southern slice into a Calgary riding it shares no schools or transit with, and that slice fails the population test. In a full read of the public submissions, 3 records ask for a link with Calgary, as the minority drew, and 6 ask for what the majority drew. The audit counts the slice as a split of a community that thins Chestermere's voice. It did not score it on the cracking test. (Technical report, section 5.8.4; reason checks, test 5.)

Red Deer — the hybrid ridings

Red Deer's ties to Blackfalds, Sylvan Lake, Lacombe and Innisfail could have grouped those towns together. The commission minority instead folds parts of the city itself into ridings led by those towns, which thins urban Red Deer's vote. In a full read of the public submissions, 1 record asks for something like that: a letter asking for the city to be split into “3-4 separate ridings” with nearby towns. 20 ask for two ridings inside the city, as the majority drew.

St. Albert — competing configurations

Either map's version of St. Albert could rest on a reasonable basis. In a full read of the public submissions, no record asks for the commission minority's change (part of the city in Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland), and none asks for the majority's change. 22 ask to keep the pattern both maps keep: one riding inside the city and one shared with Sturgeon County. The submissions give no clear signal for either map here.

Calgary north & east — larger districts, no packing signature

The commission's majority raised this in words (final report, pp. 10 and 63); the audit put a number on it. In Calgary's north and east (the audit's “Zone A”, north and east of the Bow River), the minority map's ridings average about 11.5% more people than the provincial average, against 2.8% on the majority map. So each ballot there counts for a little less. That is a finding about equal population. It does not meet the audit's packing test. Packing means crowding one party's voters into a few ridings it wins by huge margins. On 2023 votes the NDP would win 8 of the zone's 17 ridings, but by margins below the provincial average, and the test needs margins well above it. The audit measures what a map does, not why. (Technical report, section 5.3.1.)

Recommendation 5 areas

When the government handed the map to a committee of MLAs, it cited the commission chair's Recommendation 5. That recommendation sets four conditions for a 91-seat map. Three of them name ridings on the majority map. Each area is described below, with the majority-map ridings it covers.

Recommendation 5(a): no change from Airdrie south

The chair’s Recommendation 5 says restoring the two rural seats should not touch any riding in Airdrie, or with land south of Airdrie’s southern city limit, except Drumheller-Stettler. That is 41 ridings, every Calgary seat among them, that should match the majority map line for line. Airdrie and Calgary are where the commission split most sharply; this condition keeps them as the majority drew them.

Majority-map districts in this area: Airdrie-East, Airdrie-West, Calgary-Acadia, Calgary-Beddington, Calgary-Bhullar-McCall, Calgary-Bow, Calgary-Buffalo, Calgary-Confluence, Calgary-Cross, Calgary-Currie, Calgary-East, Calgary-Edgemont, Calgary-Elbow, Calgary-Falconridge-Conrich, Calgary-Fish Creek, Calgary-Glenmore-Tsuut'ina, Calgary-Hays, Calgary-Klein, Calgary-Lougheed, Calgary-McKenzie, Calgary-Mountain View, Calgary-North, Calgary-North East, Calgary-North West, Calgary-Nose Creek, Calgary-Shaw, Calgary-South East, Calgary-Symons Valley, Calgary-Varsity, Calgary-West-Elbow Valley, Canmore-Banff, Chestermere-Strathmore, Cochrane-Springbank, High River-Vulcan-Siksika, Lethbridge-East, Lethbridge-West, Livingstone-Macleod, Medicine Hat-Brooks, Medicine Hat-Cypress, Okotoks-Diamond Valley, Taber-Cardston.

Recommendation 5(b): no change in north Edmonton

Recommendation 5 says restoring the two rural seats should not touch any Edmonton riding north of the North Saskatchewan River. That is 11 ridings that should match the majority map. Beverly-Clareview crosses the river; about two-thirds of it is on the north bank, so it is counted here.

Majority-map districts in this area: Edmonton-Beverly-Clareview, Edmonton-Castle Downs, Edmonton-City Centre, Edmonton-Decore, Edmonton-Enoch, Edmonton-Glenora-Riverview, Edmonton-Highlands-Norwood, Edmonton-Manning, Edmonton-McClung, Edmonton-North West, Edmonton-West Henday.

Recommendation 5(c): south Edmonton goes back to the draft

Recommendation 5 says every Edmonton riding south of the North Saskatchewan River should return to the commission’s October 2025 interim map, the draft it published for comment before the final split. One option is allowed: adding Nisku to Edmonton-South East, to put Leduc and Beaumont back in one riding. This outline marks the 10 majority-map ridings that would change. It does not show the interim lines themselves.

Majority-map districts in this area: Edmonton-Beaumont, Edmonton-Ellerslie, Edmonton-Gold Bar, Edmonton-Meadows, Edmonton-Mill Woods, Edmonton-Rutherford, Edmonton-South, Edmonton-Strathcona, Edmonton-Whitemud, Edmonton-Windermere.

Recommendation 5(d): the rural seat to restore

Justice Dallas Miller, the commission’s chair, wrote that if the Legislature would not accept losing two rural ridings, it should go from 89 seats to 91 and restore them. This is the one place he named for a restored seat: a riding made mostly of Clearwater County and western Mountain View County, with s. 15(2) status (the rule that lets a large, remote riding have far fewer people than average). He asked that it be considered, not required. The outline is approximate: Clearwater County plus the part of Mountain View County that sat in the 2019 Rimbey-Rocky Mountain House-Sundre riding. It lies wholly north of Airdrie and takes no land from any riding conditions (a) or (b) protect. He named no place for the second seat.

District directory

This table lists every riding name on any of the three maps, in alphabetical order. For each name it shows whether it is on the minority map, the majority map and the 2019 map. Names differ between maps, so some rows appear on only one or two. The table gives each name, which maps have it, and the riding's 2023 vote total counting all ballots. For populations and test results, see the full report.

Each figure is the riding's 2023 vote total on that map, counting all ballots. Elections Alberta reports advance, mobile and special ballots only for each 2023 riding. So the audit spreads them over that riding's voting areas in proportion to the election-day votes each area cast. Each old riding's total therefore matches the official result, but how it splits between new ridings is an estimate. A newer rule, rule P, not yet adopted, would split some of them differently; the full record gives its results. A dash (—) means the riding name is not on that map.

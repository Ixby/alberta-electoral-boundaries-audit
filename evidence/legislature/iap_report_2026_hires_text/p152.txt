Minority Opinion - Panel of the Special Select Committee on Electoral Boundaries  33

128. Our colleagues have based their use of hybrid electoral divisions on the idea that Lethbridge is the centre
     of a type of "node" that is used by people all over Southern Alberta. We do not doubt that people from
     all over Southern Alberta frequent Lethbridge for a variety of reasons. We suspect this is the same way
     that people from various parts of Alberta use Calgary or Edmonton for various services as large urban
     centres tend to offer services and interactions that are not economically viable in smaller centres. However,
     this does not mean that the people of Lethbridge intrinsically see these people as part of their community
     nor does it mean that the people visiting Lethbridge see themselves as part of that community. This was
     expressed loud and clear in the public engagement on this matter when an overwhelming majority of the
     residents of Lethbridge stated they opposed this type of electoral division structure. The small number of
     voices in favour were those from outside Lethbridge or those with a vested interest in this structure.

129. As an example of their approach, our colleagues justify dividing Lethbridge in part on the basis that
     Southern Alberta shares health care services such as the Chinook Hospital. However, the Livingstone-
     Macleod and Taber-Cardston MLAs should be advocating for Lethbridge health services whether or not
     Lethbridge is in their electoral divisions, because their constituents use the services. This is just like how
     the Lethbridge-West MLA should be advocating for the hospital even though it is located in Lethbridge-
     East. The same is true of the post-secondary institutions. But residents throughout Southern Alberta
     (especially outside Lethbridge County) do not share interests regarding schools, roads, and myriad other
     matters we note at paragraph 127. They are in different municipal governments and generally have
     significantly different housing structures and municipal taxation considerations. This also distinguishes our
     colleagues' proposed hybridization of Lethbridge from the hybrids they (and, to a lesser extent we) propose
     concerning Enoch, Nisku, Tsuut'ina, Prince of Peace, Conrich, Bearspaw, Springbank, and Elbow Valley.
     In those cases, the residents use the schools and other municipal services from the cities. One can fairly
     say these areas are functionally in Calgary or Edmonton. One could even say the same regarding the areas
     of Lethbridge County in acreages immediately to the city's east or west: but this ends once one leaves these
     areas within a ten-minute drive, and certainly once one leaves Lethbridge County. We also note that our
     colleagues' proposed electoral division of Lethbridge-Little Bow extends north of the Siksika Reserve such
     that residents would be travelling to Calgary rather than Lethbridge for government services.

130. Although a case could be made that municipalities such as Coaldale and Coalhurst share interests and
     outlooks with residents of Lethbridge, removing the population of these municipalities from their rural
     neighbouring electoral divisions creates cascading effects that are clearly unreasonable, such as residents
     of Lethbridge being paired with residents of areas north of the Siksika Reserve or in the County of 40
     Mile. Once one goes further into rural Alberta, and certainly once one leaves Lethbridge County, there is
     very little support for, and significant resistance to and doubts about, hybridizing various parts of Southern
     Alberta with Lethbridge. This approach to hybridization is treated just as skeptically among rural and urban
     voters. The reactions of Okotoks's residents and municipal politicians to being paired with Calgary in the
     ABEBC Interim Report were telling of how most urban and rural Albertans view these matters.

131. We also view the agricultural link in this area to be of minimal importance in drawing electoral boundaries.
     Agriculture is the sixth largest employer by industry classification in the entire Lethbridge-Medicine Hat
     region with 11,500 employees in June, 2026 out of 155,100 total employed persons. Accordingly, our
     colleagues have sought to override what seems to be the vast popular opinion of the submissions provided
     by the residents of the area to try to unite the common interests of less than 10% of the region's workforce.

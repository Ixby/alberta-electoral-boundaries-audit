5. Commission Chairman's Recommendation 5

The Commission Chairman's Recommendation 5 was contained in an Addendum to the Majority Report in
the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission's Final Report. It was endorsed only by the Chairman and
has been reproduced in the Appendix to the advisory panel's report.

Some of our panel members take the position that implementing, not simply incorporating Recommendation
5 in other proposals the advisory panel might make, was the panel's only mandate. Recommendation 5 was
argued to be our pole star and nothing else. It was characterized by the minority members as something in
the nature of a commandment from the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission Chairman.

It was not. But let us have a closer look.

The Chairman prefaced his Recommendation 5 with his Commission's regret at having to recommend the
removal of two rural seats.

The Chairman then went on to state that it was his duty to uphold the law, an allusion to the majority's
criticism of the minority's recommendations which he considered unlawful.

The Chairman then characterized the Majority Report as "the product of trade-offs, compromises, and give
and take". "Putting water in your wine" was a phrase he used. By that, the Chairman appears to concede that
the law permits electoral boundaries commissions to engage in what he termed trade-offs, compromises,
give and take or putting water in your wine. We agree.

The Chairman then made a proposal which he stated was made for the express purpose of dissuading
the Legislature from accepting the Minority Report. In other words, the give and take continued in
Recommendation 5. The Chairman was now bargaining with the Legislature.

The Chairman's proposal was this: if the Legislature finds that it cannot concur in the majority's proposal
to remove two electoral divisions, then the Legislature ought to increase the number of electoral divisions
and restore the two that were removed. The quid pro quo (the trade-off) was that the rest of the Province's
electoral divisions be maintained to the extent possible. And in making his proposal the Chairman went
on to suggest a number of restrictions he thought the restorations "should" meet. These restrictions were
also intended to preserve proposals the Commission had made. However, the Chairman conceded that
his recommendation, including the restrictions, might need to be studied by an all-party Committee of
the Legislature.

In short, the Chairman, in effect, was propositioning the Legislature, proposing a bargain for its
consideration, that he would support the restoration of two rural ridings if the Legislature would refrain from
approving any of the minority recommendations.

The Legislature accepted some of the Chairman's proposed plan, but not all of it, at least not yet. The
Legislature might be said to have put a little water in the Chairman's wine.

The Legislature did increase its size to 91 seats and approved the restoration of the two rural electoral
divisions. But before agreeing to approve the majority's recommendations, the Legislature wanted the rest of
the Province reviewed, not with a view to protecting the Commission's proposed electoral divisions, but with a
view to having an independent panel of advisors review the Province's electoral divisions and make proposals.

As an advisory panel on electoral boundaries, we were cognizant of the fact that Section 11 of the Electoral
Boundaries Commission Act contemplates the Legislative Assembly approving an electoral boundaries
commission's proposals "with alterations". We saw our task as one of identifying alterations which might
better achieve effective representation.

Calgary-Bow-Springbank

One of the more difficult decisions the advisory panel had to make was what to recommend with respect
to Springbank. It is currently (2017) part of the Banff-Kananaskis electoral division which makes no sense.
The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission proposed that it be put in a Cochrane electoral division
which made more sense. The advisory panel proposes that Springbank be paired with either Calgary-Bow
or Calgary-West depending on what side of the TransCanada Highway its residents live. The connection
between Springbank and Cochrane is clearly not as strong as it once was and certainly not as strong as
Springbank's connection to Calgary.

We were not all agreed, but the majority proposes that the Legislature consider putting North Springbank
in the Calgary-Bow electoral division and South Springbank into the Calgary-West electoral division. North
Springbank being that portion of Springbank north of the TransCanada Highway and South Springbank being
that portion south of the TransCanada Highway.

The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission clearly considered recommending this in their review
of the Calgary-Bow electoral division. And, as indicated, minority members of the 2025-2026 Electoral
Boundaries Commission actually did recommend that the Calgary-Bow electoral division be extended west
to include Springbank. But, in its Final Report, the majority members stated that they chose not to include
Springbank in this electoral division because it would make the population too large. The advisory panel
respectfully disagrees. Including Springbank in the electoral division results in a population of roughly 9%
above the average electoral division's population for all 91 of the Province's electoral divisions. That is well
within the permitted variances.

Submissions to the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission pointed to the indisputable fact that
substantial portions of Springbank are occupied by residents in suburban or acreage communities which
have been closely connected to Calgary for decades.

The Chair of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission, in his Addendum to the Majority Report,
stated that he did not consider what he referred to as the "hybridization" of Springbank with Calgary
"necessarily unreasonable or procedurally unfair given the extensive submissions about them". The
Chairman went on to state that, but for what he characterized as the minority's unreasonable insistence on
other hybrids and their "radical about face and substantive unreasonableness", he might well have agreed
with the minority including Springbank in a Calgary electoral division.

All of this is simply to indicate that including Springbank in Calgary Bow is worthy of consideration by an
advisory panel which has been directed to review the Province's electoral divisions. It is not, as has been
alleged, unconstitutional gerrymandering, nor is it clearly outside the advisory panel's mandate. Indeed, it
was prompted by the Commission Chair's comments in his Addendum to the Majority Report.

But adding Springbank to Calgary Bow did present the advisory panel with other problems it had to deal with.

First, it is argued that adding Springbank to Calgary Bow may have implications for voter outcomes. All
electoral boundary changes have the potential to affect voter outcomes. Adding Springbank to Calgary
Bow is said to potentially favour the party in power. But whether that potential will manifest itself in the next
election, not even the pollsters with their voter transpositioning software know for sure.

Second, there is the issue of defining with precision what geographical area should be added. Springbank,
as Calgarians generally understand it, consists of a number of suburban and acreage subdivisions west of
Calgary on both sides of the TransCanada Highway.

For the purposes of the advisory panel's recommendations, Springbank is defined on the west by the north-
south Highway 22 connecting Bragg Creek to Cochrane. Its northern boundary is the Bow River. Its southern
boundary is Highway 8. And its eastern boundary is the City of Calgary.

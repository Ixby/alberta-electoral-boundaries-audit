[27] Since a legislature is always trying to accomplish some public policy goal, it is therefore

68. In our view, six contextual and purposive clues support our interpretation that the Panel's ability to deviate
     from Recommendation 5 remains connected to the restoration of the two rural electoral divisions:

1. it places us more firmly in constitutional waters;
2. it better complies with Charter values;
3. it better complies with the timeline and support granted to the Panel to complete its task;
4. it mitigates the legislature's abrogation of common law procedural fairness;
5. we would have expected clearer language to overcome the common law doctrines of collateral attack

    and abuse of process; and
6. this is consistent with statements of the government caucus, Premier, and her Chief of Staff that were

    contemporaneous to the passing of Motion 37.

While none of these are determinative on their own, we are comfortable taking them together to come to
our interpretation of our mandate. We expand upon each of them below.

69. First, we are more comfortable with an interpretation of the Panel's mandate that is similar to previous
     constitutionally irregular but constitutionally condoned processes. Making further changes to Lethbridge
     and Calgary creates significant constitutional risk, as we discuss below. While the presumption of
     constitutionality is a secondary principle of statutory interpretation, it informs our analysis in the case of
     ambiguity: Piekut v Canada (National Revenue), 2025 SCC 13 at para 48. Accordingly, it is our view
     that the constitutionality of this Panel's mandate is linked to the content of that mandate.

70. The Nova Scotia Reference is informative in this regard. In that case, a minister's interference with the
     Commission deprived commissioners of their ability to truly grapple with and assess effective
     representation. By analogy, if this Panel, with our procedural limitations, interprets the legislature's motion
     as carte blanche to redraw any boundaries as the panelists see fit--as a do-over in select areas of the
     province--then where is the Commission's ability to consider the factors relevant to effective
     representation? The IAP Majority asserts that we should not be bound by the Commission's
     recommendations. Given that we are bound by Recommendation 5, we disagree. In any event, from the
     perspective of epistemic humility, it seems unreasonable that the IAP has a better understanding of the
     communities of interest than the ABEBC given the privileged and independent status of the ABEBC.

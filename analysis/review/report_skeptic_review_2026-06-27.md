# Adversarial skeptic review of the report (2026-06-27)

Multi-agent swarm: 10 domain-specific hostile skeptics → fair rebuttal-check of every fatal/serious objection (does the report already defend it?) → synthesis of surviving objections. 43 agents, ~3.6M tokens. 32 serious/fatal objections raised; 3 fully answered by the report; 29 survive (most are public-vs-academic prose gaps + reproducibility-artifact staleness, not breaks in the core finding).

## How well the report withstands adversarial scrutiny

The headline survives as an **ordinal** claim and dies as a **precise** one. Across eight skeptic domains and dozens of objections, none lands a blow that reverses the core directional finding — the minority map is more extreme than every neutral plan sampled, and three swing-independent partisan metrics (mean-median p99.98, declination p98.79, seats@50/50 p99.99) plus the structural lane survive the corrections, the symmetric majority comparison, and the partial constraint-enforcing run. What does not survive is the marketing around that finding: the quoted "1 in 714,000 / 1 in 357,000," its reproducibility, the "neutral/legal maps" framing, and several public-facing prose claims that exceed the academic text. The **single most dangerous objection is the fatal precision-extrapolation**: the headline p = 1.40×10⁻⁶ is a pure χ²(4) parametric tail evaluated at D²=32.67, which lies *beyond* the ensemble's empirical maximum of 27.74 (0 of 1,010,000 plans reach it), and the report's own Appendix G already concedes the figure "cannot be reliably extrapolated." An objection the report quotes against itself, while still headlining the number it disowns, is the cleanest hostile-expert hit. Close behind sits a deeper problem raised independently by five domains — the null is admittedly the *wrong reference class* and the "conservative lower bound" rescue is asserted, not proven. The most acutely embarrassing are two reproducibility failures that **stand outright**.

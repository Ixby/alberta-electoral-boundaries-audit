The Legislature responded by resolution, Government Motion 37, urging the Government to introduce a
Bill amending Section 13 of the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act to provide for 91 electoral divisions
instead of 89. The resolution also called for a further independent review of the Province's electoral divisions,
including the restoration of the two deleted electoral divisions.

The addition of two more electoral divisions made a further review of the current (2017) electoral divisions and
those recommended by the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission necessary. Adding two electoral
divisions is like adding two pieces to an already-completed jig saw puzzle. Adjustments had to be made. And
so the Legislative Assembly established a Select Special Committee on Electoral Boundaries to oversee an
independent review of the electoral boundaries of Alberta. The Legislative Assembly directed the Committee
to engage independent advisors to conduct that review and make proposals.

The Legislative Assembly directed that the independent advisory panel, in conducting its review of the Province's
electoral divisions, divide the Province into 91 electoral divisions, and make proposals as to their boundaries and
areas, and incorporate in those proposals the Chairman's recommendations found within Recommendation 5 in
the Addendum to the Majority Report. Incorporating the recommendations in Recommendation 5 was expressly
made subject to any modifications the independent advisory panel considered necessary.

Having conducted its review of the electoral boundaries of Alberta; having made proposals as to the area,
boundaries and names of the electoral divisions; and having incorporated the recommendation of the 2025-
2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission Chairman, the advisory panel was to submit to the Select Special
Committee of the Legislature on Electoral Boundaries a report based on its review and proposals.

The Select Special Committee was then to review the advisory panel's report, including making
"observations" in respect of it. At the conclusion of its review, the Select Special Committee was to submit
the independent advisory panel's report to the Legislature. And in due course, the Legislature will presumably
consider the advisory panel's report, the Commission's report and any other information the Legislature
considers relevant in order to make a decision on the areas, boundaries and names of the Province's electoral
divisions for the next general election.

That electoral boundary determination is an iterative process might be illustrated by the following list of the
steps required to be taken in order to set Alberta's electoral boundaries:

    1. Public hearings
    2. Interim report
    3. More public hearings
    4. Final report with the Commission's proposals
    5. The Commission Chairman's separate recommendations
    6. Legislature's review of Commission Report and Chair's recommendations
    7. Legislature's resolution to increase number of seats to 91
    8. Legislature appoints Committee of the Legislature and independent advisory panel to review, make

         proposals and to incorporate Chairman's recommendations
    9. Advisory panel conducts its review
    10. Advisory panel submits report based on its review
    11. Legislative Committee convenes to review report and comment on it
    12.Legislative Committee submits advisory panel's report and Legislative Committee's observations

         to the Legislature
    13. Legislature considers and deliberates

None of the foregoing deliberative steps support the allegation that the process is unconstitutional.

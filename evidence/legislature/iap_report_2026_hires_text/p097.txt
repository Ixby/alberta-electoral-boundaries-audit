4. Mandate of the Independent Advisory Panel

Far too much of the advisory panel's time and energy was spent debating the panel's mandate.

The advisory panel's mandate was clearly set out in Clause C of Government Motion 37.

In Clause C.(a), the Legislature established a Select Special Committee of the Legislature on Electoral
Boundaries "to oversee an independent review of electoral boundaries of Alberta". The significance of the
word "independent" may not be clear; but it could reasonably be interpreted to mean an ab initio review (i.e.
a new review), a trial de novo, so to speak.

In Clause C.(e)(i) the advisory panel's mandate was to "conduct a review of the electoral boundaries of
Alberta" and "make proposals as to the area, boundaries and names of the electoral divisions of Alberta".
These words are identical to the words used in Section 3 of the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act to
describe the "function "of an electoral boundaries commission. Enacted by the same Legislature, the
words used in Motion 37 could not possibly mean any less than what they mean in the Electoral Boundaries
Commission Act.

In Clause C. (e)(ii)(A), the panel was to "divide" the Province into 91 proposed electoral divisions.

In Clause C.(e)(ii)(B), having approved the Chairman's recommendations to increase the number of electoral
divisions from 89 to 91 and having also accepted his proposal to restore the deleted electoral divisions,
the Legislature directed the advisory panel to "incorporate" (into whatever other proposals the advisory
panel might make) the Commission Chairman's Recommendation 5 subject to any modifications the panel
considered necessary.

The advisory panel did not sit in appeal of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commissions
recommendations. Nor was the panel bound by the Commission's proposals. Nor was it mandated to
defend, uphold or adopt the Commission's proposals. The advisory panel's process was not intended to be
simply a continuation of the Electoral Boundaries Commission's process. The panel was mandated to review
the existing electoral boundaries of Alberta, which were set in the 2017 Electoral Divisions Act, and make
proposals to the Legislature as to their area, boundaries and names.

That said, the advisory panel's review of the Province's electoral boundaries took into consideration and
often deferred to the 2025-2026 Commission's recommendations. That is, the panel considered all of
the Commission's proposals: the majority's proposals, the minority's proposals and, as mandated by the
Legislature, the Chairman's proposals. In deciding whether to make proposals for the area, boundaries and
names of electoral divisions, deference was given to the proposals of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries
Commission, except where some or all of the advisory panel's members were of the view that a different
area or boundary of an electoral division might better achieve effective representation based on recognized
and statutorily prescribed considerations.

A "review" involves an assessment of merits, and an assessment can trigger "proposals". Government Motion
37 expressly mandated the panel to "review" the Province's electoral boundaries and make "proposals".

Conspicuous by its absence in the minority's interpretation of the advisory panel's mandate is any
consideration of the effect of the mandate in Clause C. (e)(i) to make "proposals" as to the area, boundaries,
and names of the electoral divisions of Alberta. The Legislature expressly mandated the advisory panel to
make those proposals, not simply rubber-stamp what two or even three members of the 2025-2026 Electoral
Boundaries Commission proposed.

The minority's opinion never does parse the actual wording of Government Motion 37 preferring, instead,
to base their interpretation of the panel's mandate on their characterization of the "context" and "purpose"
of the Motion. They ignore the admonishment of Justice Stratas whom they quote that text must be the
"anchor" of any interpretive exercise.

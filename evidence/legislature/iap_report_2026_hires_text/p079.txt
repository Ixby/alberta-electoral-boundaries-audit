The Case for Regional Representation in Southern Alberta

The advisory panel's recommendations for southern Alberta are driven by two recognized electoral
boundary-making considerations. The first is communities of interest: Southern Alberta is uniquely united
economically around the agricultural and food processing industries which transcend the boundaries of the
City of Lethbridge. The second consideration is population, the guiding principle or standard of reference in
defining the geographic areas of electoral divisions.

Guided by these two considerations, the advisory panel recommends a distinct Lethbridge electoral
division. It also recommends organizing the surrounding regions around three coherent hybrid electoral
divisions, borrowing population from the perimeters of the City of Lethbridge to achieve relative population
parity and to reflect shared community interests. Southern Alberta is united economically around the
agricultural and food processing industry which transcend the City of Lethbridge boundaries. The advisory
panel recommends the following:

   1. A re-established Lethbridge-Little Bow electoral division including a portion of northeast Lethbridge
      and a bit of west Lethbridge to give the electoral division necessary population.

   2. A re-established Lethbridge-Taber-Warner electoral division including a portion of east Lethbridge
      (the agricultural fairgrounds and communities north of it) in order to provide the necessary population
      that the current (2017) electoral division would lose as a result of the panel recommending a separate
      electoral division for Cardston. Taber-Warner has been below average in population since at least the
      2017 Electoral Boundaries Commission review.

   3. A re-established Lethbridge-Cardston electoral division for Cardston, Raymond, and Magrath and
      the largest First Nations Reserve in Canada, the Kainai Blood Reserve headquartered at Stand Off. In
      order to give Cardston the population needed to be in a separate electoral division, the 2017 Electoral
      Boundaries Commission created an electoral division extending from Cardston to points north of
      the Bow River to the TransCanada Highway, including the Siksika Reserve. Population has been a
      problem for a Cardston electoral division as well. Our recommendation to solve the problem is to have
      Lethbridge lend a little population to Cardston.

   4. A re-established Fort Macleod electoral division west of Lethbridge, Fort Macleod and Pincher Creek
      along Highway 3 to the Municipality of Crowsnest and north along Highway 2 to High River, taking
      in Claresholm, Stavely and Nanton. It would no longer surround Lethbridge if the advisory panel's
      proposals are approved. And Coaldale, Picture Butte and Turin are all proposed to be removed.

The actual or precise recommended boundaries have been dictated by the need to maintain each electoral
division within the statutorily mandated population variances and by the panel's desire to recommend
electoral divisions with similar communities of interest that follow clear and recognizable features.

Southern Alberta is currently divided into five electoral divisions, including an electoral division which runs
from Cardston to the Siksika Reserve east of Calgary. The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission
in turn also divided southern Alberta into five electoral divisions, one of which was proposed to run
from Claresholm to the Red Deer River. The panel is likewise proposing five electoral divisions. What is
being proposed is not that much different than what has been in place since at least 2010 and what was
recommended by both the 2017 and 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commissions. What is different is that
Lethbridge is being asked to share some of its population with adjoining electoral divisions in order to ensure
that they can remain primarily southern Alberta constituencies. That is, the reason the panel recommends
these changes is to achieve better regional representation of the people of southern Alberta.

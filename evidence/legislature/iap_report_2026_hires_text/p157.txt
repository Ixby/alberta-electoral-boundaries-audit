152. Since the Alberta Court of Appeal made it clear in the 1994 Reference that significant variances in
     populations between electoral divisions must decrease, we have seen a gradual reduction in population
     variances. The ABEBC Majority Report reversed a decades-long trend in this respect. The IAP Majority
     indicates that population disparities must further increase in the future.

153. While population is only one statutory consideration in drawing electoral divisions, it remains the prime
     variable in drawing electoral divisions. While not the end of the analysis concerning effective
     representation, it is sound to treat all Albertans equally in the absence of a compelling reason to the
     contrary. Ian Gray made this point particularly well in a submission in response to the Interim Report:

           The Commission has provided its detailed explanation as to why Alberta (and Canada) prefers
           the principle of "effective representation" over strict voter parity. It's well reasoned and logical
           and the Commission must be recognized for striving mightily to keep the variances in its report
           as low as it can (under 10% + or minus, in the majority of ridings). But it doesn't make the
           situation any less irksome to an Albertan whose vote is diluted compared to someone
           else's. A vote in the proposed Edmonton-Glenora-Riverview riding will be worth roughly 32%
           less than a vote from Drumheller-Stettler. While fully recognizing the constraints under which
           it must operate, anything the Commission can do to get that number and others like it, closer to
           parity, serves the interests of democracy. [emphasis added]

Mr. Gray, to be sure, admitted he was trying to "have [his] cake and eat it too" (his words) by seeking to
preserve the electoral division of Edmonton-Riverview. But he makes a good point: before we tell an
Albertan that their vote should be worth less than another's, we must have a very good reason--ideally, a
non-partisan one that almost all people of goodwill will accept as reasonable. Once the reason given is
subject to reasonable disagreement, or appears to be partisan gamesmanship in disguise, many Albertans
will immediately suspect that their vote is being diluted unnecessarily. Little could undermine Alberta
democracy more. These concerns are heightened in the aftermath of the ABEBC Minority Report.

Macleod-Crowsnest

Macleod-Crowsnest remains a huge electoral division west of Lethbridge. It runs from Fort Macleod west
to the Crowsnest Pass along Highway 3, including Pincher Creek and Waterton Lakes National Park. From
Fort Macleod north to High River up Highway 2, including Granum, Claresholm, Stavely, Nanton and Blackie.
And north from Lundbreck up Highway 22 to the Chain Lakes Provincial Park and the south boundary of
Kananaskis Country. It includes the Piikani First Nation Reserve at Brocket. It is bounded on the south end
west by the U.S. and B.C. borders respectively. It is bounded on the east by the Waterton, Belly and Little Bow
Rivers. The electoral division's east boundary heads southeast from Monarch along the Belly and Waterton
Rivers on the west side of the Blood Reserve.
It looks a lot like the current (2017) Livingstone-Macleod electoral division. The 2025-2026 Electoral
Boundaries Commission proposed to change it substantially, making it a more east-west than a north-south
electoral division. The Commission mapped its northern boundary south of Claresholm along Highway
520 and along the north boundary of Lethbridge County. But in order to draw down the electoral division's
north boundary, the Commission had to push its eastern boundary further east to take in Coaldale, Picture
Butte, Turin such that the Livingston-Macleod electoral division the Commission recommended would have
surrounded the City of Lethbridge on three sides, taking in Coalhurst and Coaldale and territory to the east
and southeast.
The configuration the advisory panel proposes is admittedly driven by its Lethbridge and Little Bow
recommendations north of Lethbridge and by its Taber-Warner recommendations east of Lethbridge. But
in the advisory panel's view the proposed electoral division is preferable to that recommended by the
2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission. It preserves the status quo and avoids the incongruous
encirclement of the City of Lethbridge that the Commission's recommendation entailed.
It is not the most desirable electoral division in the Province because of its size and shape. However, it has
always been thus and the people living in it understand it. Many also understand the reason for its size is to
capture the required population.
Those living in the electoral division share primarily agricultural-related communities of interest, although
those living next to Livingstone Range on the west have serious mining and resource extraction concerns.
Many of the resource extraction activities in the area were once strictly governed by the Government's
Eastern Slopes Policy. Controversies over oil and gas drilling and coal mining continue to this day. But the
communities of interest are well defined.

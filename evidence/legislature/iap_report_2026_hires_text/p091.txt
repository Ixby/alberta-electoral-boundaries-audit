Lethbridge-Cardston

Cardston is currently in an electoral division that runs from Waterton Lakes National Park to the Siksika
Nation and Gleichen east of Calgary. It is called Cardston-Siksika and is a large hourglass shaped electoral
division running from the U.S. border to the TransCanada Highway east of Calgary. It was driven by the
2017 Electoral Boundaries Commission's "consolidation of seven existing (2010) electoral divisions into six
because of the lower rates of population growth experienced... in southeastern Alberta."
The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission recommended what it characterized as "significant
changes" to the boundaries of Taber-Warner and Cardston-Siksika also to reflect changes in the distribution
of the population of Southern Alberta. What is being alluded to is that while Lethbridge is growing rapidly, the
surrounding areas are not. The Commission's solution to the lower growth rates outside of Lethbridge was
to recommend a previous electoral division configuration which combined Cardston with Taber and Warner,
including Bow Island and Foremost. Certainly not a bad fix, but whether that configuration can withstand the
Province's population increases for any length of time may be questionable.
Another solution to this population disparity problem is the creation of a Cardston, Magrath, Raymond, and
Blood Reserve electoral division that borrows population from the City of Lethbridge (roughly 17,000 people).
Lethbridge is already almost too large to be limited to only two representatives in the Legislature. The
advisory panel's recommendation might better withstand the test of time and future population increases
than the perfectly understandable recommendation of the Commission.
Borrowing population to create electoral divisions of a manageable size is legitimate electoral mapping.
There are precedents for it throughout Alberta. In fact, it's the rule, rather than the exception for dozens of
electoral divisions bordering or encompassing the Province's small towns and cities. There are only two
cities outside of Edmonton and Calgary that are not already sharing population with or encompassed by
rural populations.
But, to optimize effective representation, there must also be recognizable communities of interests in
these hybrid ridings. Perhaps nowhere else in Alberta is there a clearer shared community of interests
than in Southern Alberta around Lethbridge. There are lots of other agricultural communities of interest
in Alberta but those which exist around Lethbridge are arguably more impactful on the day to day lives of
the people both inside and outside of Lethbridge than they are elsewhere. It may also be a more inward-
focused community of interests because of the distance between Southern Alberta and the Government in
Edmonton. As evidence of that, at one point, the panel even considered making Cardston a special electoral
division under Section 15(2) of the Act. The point is that a solution needs to be found. The Commission
recognized that and so does the advisory panel. The advisory panel simply recommends a different solution
and that the Legislature seriously consider it.

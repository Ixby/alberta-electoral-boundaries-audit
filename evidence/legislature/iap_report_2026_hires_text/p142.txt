90. These electoral divisions in Table 1 are based on the rationale that areas such as Tsuut'ina Nation, Conrich,
     Prince of Peace, Elbow Valley, Nisku, and Enoch Cree Nation are functionally more integrated into the
     cities of Calgary and Edmonton than they are to surrounding rural regions.

91. Our colleagues further propose two additional hybrids of this nature. The first, Calgary-Bow-Springbank,
     will extend approximately 30 km from Calgary's western border and move the northern half of the
     community of Springbank in Rocky View County from the Cochrane-Springbank electoral division (as
     created by the ABEBC Majority, thus splitting Springbank, as the southern half of Springbank is to be
     moved to Calgary-West-Elbow Valley). The second, Calgary-North West, will extend 4 km from Calgary's
     border and shift the community of Bearspaw Village, also located in Rocky View County, into a Calgary
     electoral division. While we disagree that these changes are "necessary" to achieve effective representation,
     we accept that they bear some similarities to the six electoral divisions where we are unanimous.

92. For all eight of these electoral divisions (6 connected to Calgary, 2 connected to Edmonton), the rural
     populations added to the urban districts in the large cities are relatively small compared to the overall
     populations of the electoral divisions, such that urban representation (which is already a disproportionately
     small share of the legislature) is not diluted further.

93. We consider this a defensible approach to hybrid electoral divisions on the basis of communities of interest
     (which we unpack further below). To be frank, the one of us who has experience in municipal governance
     in the Calgary area is actually skeptical of this type of hybrid electoral division: people who choose to live
     outside the city usually have consciously chosen not to be part of the city. Government services are also
     offered in a very different way once one crosses the city border. Nonetheless, we (especially the law
     professor) do not deny that, in theory, this is a defensible approach to drawing electoral divisions. To use
     an obvious example, virtually everyone (except those opposed to any hybrid electoral divisions in Calgary
     and Edmonton) agreed that Enoch Cree Nation should be in an Edmonton electoral division, rather than
     an electoral division with Drayton Valley. Enoch Cree Nation is clearly more integrated in, and culturally
     akin to, Edmonton than Drayton Valley. The "choice" to live on reserve or in Edmonton is also

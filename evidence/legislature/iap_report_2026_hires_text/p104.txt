understanding of the electoral boundary determination process. Instead, they preferred to stoke the flames
of controversy.

And now the minority members of the advisory panel are attacking this panel's process in similar terms.
They purport to be non-partisan. They are not. Their partisanship is founded on defending the proposals
of the majority of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission at all costs. They admit as much: "We
[the minority] essentially view this Panel's work as a continuation and completion of the AEBC's work." This
despite a clear mandate from the Legislature to do otherwise.

It was not the panel's function or the function of any of its members to defend the proposals of either the
majority or the minority of the recent Electoral Boundaries Commission. The panel's function was to review
the Province's electoral boundaries and make its own independent and impartial proposals with respect to
their boundaries, areas and names.

Attacking the process of the independent advisory panel as being constitutionally irregular or suggesting that
its proposals are manifestly unconstitutional is simply a continuation of the controversy triggered by some of
the more inflammatory allegations in the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission's "Response to the
Minority Report".

So a defence of not only the advisory panel process, but also the process of electoral boundary commissions
generally, became a necessary, but counter-productive diversion from what was supposed to be the advisory
panel's mandated task. The electoral boundaries commission process needs defending because irresponsible
and inflammatory criticism, encouraged by the Commission's criticisms of its minority, and now by the
criticisms of two of our panel members, is unfairly bringing the process into disrepute.

In March of this year, the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission Final Report was tabled in the
Legislature. A recommendation was contained in that report, formulated by the Chairman "for the express
purpose of dissuading the Legislature from accepting the minority report". How adding two more electoral
divisions and restoring the two deleted electoral divisions would dissuade the Legislature, one way or
the other, from considering and perhaps approving some of the minority's proposals was not clear. The
connection is not obvious and can only be understood as one of the Commission Chairman proposing
a "compromise". Compromises are sometimes required when one is seeking approval for an electoral
boundary proposal, whether the approval sought is that of one's fellow commissioners or that of the
Legislature. But we would urge those reading Recommendation 5 to recognize it for what it is: an attempt
to persuade the Legislature to approve the Majority Report instead of the Minority Report. An inducement
intended to persuade the Legislature to approve the Majority recommendations.

The Legislature was confronted with an electoral boundary commission report which contained the
Majority's proposals, the Minority's proposals, the Majority's Response to the Minority's proposals and
an independent recommendation made by the Chairman, alone. Faced with conflicting electoral division
proposals and the Chairman's suggested compromise, is it any wonder the Legislature called for a further
review? To suggest that a further review, in these circumstances, is unconstitutional or contravenes the
Charter or is inimicable to fair and effective representation is simply not supported by the facts or the law.

Nor is the review the advisory panel actually conducted, or the proposals it now makes, contrary to any
constitutional or Charter principles.

The advisory panel has made proposals, all of which are worthy of consideration by the Legislature,
notwithstanding that we did not all agree on all of them. The reasoned disagreements over the merits of
the panel's proposals will assist the Legislature in its assessment of their merits. What will not assist are
suggestions of exceeding mandates and/or Charter breaches. Indeed, I regret having had to respond to these
suggestions. It is simply more fodder for the bloggers and others, who take delight in inflammatory criticism
rather than meaningfully participating in the process.

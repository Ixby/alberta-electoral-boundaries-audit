Calgary-West-Springbank

The issue here is whether or not the remainder of Springbank South, or that portion of Springbank which lies
south of the TransCanada Highway and north of the Elbow River, should become part of an electoral division
comprised of Calgary West and South Springbank.

In the last general election, Calgary West was a relatively compact and understandable electoral division
between Sarcee Trail and the City limits or 101St Street S.W. It was bordered on the north by Bow Trail and on
the south by a City of Calgary boundary. It included the Calgary communities of Strathcona, Christie Park,
Signature Parke, Signal Hill and Richmond Hill along Sarcee Trail and Aspen Woods, Elbow View, Springbank
Hill and Discovery Ridge to the west. The electoral division of Banff-Kananaskis, stretching all the way from
the B.C. Border to the City of Calgary, abutted Calgary-West. Clearly the inclusion of Springbank in a Banff-
Kananaskis electoral division was less than ideal.

One change the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission recommended to address that situation was
to put some, but not all, of Springbank into an electoral division with Cochrane. That was an improvement,
but arguably, not optimal.

Evidence for that can be found in what the Commission recommended. The Commission recommended
putting an elongated east-west stretch of land south of the Elbow River straddling Highway 8 into Calgary-
West and called it Calgary-West-Elbow Valley. The Commission's Elbow Valley straddled Highway 8 (the
highway to Bragg Creek) all the way to Highway 22 (the highway between Bragg Creek and Cochrane).
The electoral division's southern border was the Tsuut'ina Nation's Reserve west of Calgary which the
Commission recommended be included in another Calgary electoral division, namely Calgary Glenmore.
The Commission called Calgary-West-Elbow Valley a hybrid electoral division which they characterized as
the "way of the future".

Whether Calgary-West-Elbow Valley was a true hybrid or not, it certainly is a way of the future. Some would
argue it was an idea long since recognized. In any event, it was yet another precedent for extending the
boundaries of a suburban electoral division beyond the boundaries of the city to encompass suburban
communities beyond those boundaries. As we have stated elsewhere, this was not a hybridization of rural
and urban populations. The electoral division does not mix or combine people of fundamentally different
interests. It is simply an electoral division which straddles a municipal boundary, uniting for electoral
purposes only, suburban residents of the City with suburban residents of Rockyview County.

We concur in what the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission in extending Calgary-West into Rocky
View County along Highway 8 and its reasons therefor. It is the independent advisory panel's view that
those reasons apply equally to the rest of South Springbank. And so, the panel recommends that the entire
area loosely referred to as South Springbank be amalgamated with Calgary-West for electoral boundary
purposes. The electoral division we propose would have a much more regular polygon shape with straight
line boundaries, as opposed to the long, narrow, meandering salamander shape of Calgary-West-Elbow
Valley recommended by the Commission.

The minority members of the panel criticize the splitting of Springbank north and south of the TransCanada
Highway. The splitting of Springbank along the Elbow River was worse.

Like Springbank north of the TransCanada Highway, substantial portions of Springbank south of the highway
are occupied by residents in urban or suburban or acreage communities which have been closely connected
to the city for decades.

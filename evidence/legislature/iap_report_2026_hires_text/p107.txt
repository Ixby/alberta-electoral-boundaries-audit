7.Population

By law, the population of a proposed electoral division must not be more than 25% above and not more
than 25% below the average population of all the proposed electoral divisions: Section 15(1) of the Electoral
Boundaries Commission Act.

As a consequence, the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act prescribes rules for determining of
the Province's population. Boundaries commissions are to use the most recent Federal Government
"decennials census". But the results of the most recent decennial census (2026) were not available to the
Commission, nor were they available to the advisory panel. They will not be released until 2027. In that
event, the Act permits an electoral boundaries commission to use "a province-wide census that is more
recent than the decennial census"; so long as it is used in conjunction with information provided in the
Federal Government's last decennial census. See Section 12 of the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act in
the Appendix to this report for the hierarchy of rules governing the determination of the population of Alberta.
The rules are dictated by what census information is available and how recent that information is.

Electoral boundaries commissions have historically struggled deciding on appropriate population figures.
The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission struggled with the issue, but aided by some very good
statisticians and demographers, they relied on the 2024 mid-year population estimates which were
released in May of 2025 or about the time the Commission got started. The Commission was unanimous in
its decision to use these population figures. The Commission used the number of 4,888,723 for Alberta's
population as the most accurate and verified population that it had at its disposal.

The advisory panel too was unanimous in using that population figure for several reasons. The first was
that the Federal Government's decennial census results will not be released until 2027. Even the mid-2025
provincial population estimates were not scheduled to be released until after the advisory panel was well
into its mandate. The advisory panel was appointed in June 2026. The mid-2025 population estimates were
not scheduled to be released until July 2026. Indeed, the advisory panel is not aware whether they have yet
been released.

Another reason for the panel to rely on the 2024 mid-year population estimates the Commission used was
so that comparisons between what the advisory panel recommended and what the 2025-2026 Electoral
Boundaries Commission recommended might be made on the basis of the same population estimates.

Of course, in seeking population parity or relative population parity, or "population balancing" as it is
sometimes called, one needs to know not only the population of the Province in order to divide it by the
number of electoral divisions for the average population of the proposed electoral division, but also the
population of the proposed electoral division being considered. The advisory panel relied on Elections
Alberta for the populations of the electoral divisions it proposed. Elections Alberta has software that
instantly produces population numbers for proposed electoral division configurations the panel was
considering. The proposed electoral boundaries can be manipulated on a computer screen, and the
resultant population instantly pops up.

Elections Alberta maps population for so-called "dissemination areas" which are geographic areas
defined by Statistics Canada for census purposes. Statistics Canada apparently has defined roughly 6,000
dissemination areas in Alberta. The Federal Government attempts to count the people in those areas
every 10 years. The Provincial Government (the Office of Statistics and Information at the Alberta Treasury
Board) then estimates growth or decline in the population of dissemination areas based on a multitude of
indices and actual counts. Elections Alberta then maps those dissemination area populations in order that
electoral boundaries commissions and others might know how many people are likely to live within a defined
geographic area.

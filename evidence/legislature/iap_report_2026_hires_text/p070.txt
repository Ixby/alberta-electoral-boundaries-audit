North of the TransCanada Highway, Springbank contains not only a number of suburban and acreage
subdivisions, but also the Springbank Airport, two golf courses, riding arenas, various light industrial
developments and a special school for promising young athletes from the City of Calgary and elsewhere.

South of the TransCanada Highway, there are also suburban and acreage subdivisions, Calaway Park (an
amusement park), a regional hockey rink, riding academies and at least four golf courses.

Significantly, on both sides of the TransCanada Highway, Springbank still contains a number of working
farms and ranches. Those farmers and ranchers might be more connected to Cochrane than Calgary, but
they are a shrinking minority.

Finally, removing Springbank north of the TransCanada Highway from the Cochrane electoral division
reduces the population of the Cochrane electoral division from roughly 6% below average to almost 13%
below average. But it gives the City of Cochrane a representative in the Legislature in the same way that
similar-sized cities such as Okotoks, Airdrie and Spruce Grove will have dedicated representation in the
Legislature if the advisory panel's proposals are approved.

That description of Springbank above makes it apparent that all of Springbank cannot be conjoined with any
single Calgary electoral division without major changes to the electoral map of Calgary. But if Springbank is
conceptually separated by the TransCanada, there are Calgary electoral divisions to which the two halves of
Springbank might be logically amalgamated.

The minority argue that dividing Springbank raises issues of community-splitting, although the TransCanada
Highway is a fairly substantial natural boundary. The Highway 22 crossing of the TransCanada, the Calaway
Park/Springbank Airport crossing and the Old Banff Coach Road crossings are the only connectors between
North Springbank and South Springbank unless you're herding cattle underneath the TransCanada via a
livestock tunnel which the builders of the TransCanada Highway provided. While there may be one or two
old farming/ranching families with relatives on both sides of the TransCanada Highway, the connection
between residents North Springbank and Calgary is as strong as their connection to their neighbours across
the Highway. That said, it is true that the Springbank school south of the Highway is attended by students
who live north of the Highway. The criticism that the advisory panel's proposal would extend the Calgary-
Bow electoral riding 30 km from Calgary's western border is disingenuous. The Calgary-West-Elbow Valley
electoral division the Commission recommend extended just as far. Those 30 km are cycled daily by scores
of Calgarians in less than an hour and are driven in about six minutes.

Another consideration in increasing the size of Calgary Bow to take in North Springbank is that some
residents of North Springbank, although not the majority, might identify more with Cochrane than Calgary.
That's a valid criticism but only for a handful of farming and ranching families.

Currently, both North and South Springbank are mapped in the Banff-Kananaskis electoral division set by
the 2017 Electoral Boundaries Commission. That was deemed unsatisfactory by the 2025-2026 Electoral
Boundaries Commission. The advisory panel agrees. The Commission put both North and South Springbank
in an electoral division with Cochrane, although significantly the Commission did not put an important
part of Springbank south of the Elbow River into the Cochrane electoral division. Instead, the Commission
attached it to Calgary West. An expanded Calgary West-Elbow Valley was recommended by the Commission
and it extended all the way west to Highway 22.

In other words, the precedent for attaching portions of these Springbank suburban/acreage areas west
of Calgary to a Calgary electoral division has already been set. We deal with Calgary-West Elbow Valley
later in this report; but suffice to say that although it is a strange-looking electoral division as the 2025-
2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission mapped it, the people of Elbow Valley, living as they do in planned
suburban subdivisions, commute to Calgary for just about all of their needs and their employment.
Conversely, residents of Calgary pour into Elbow Valley daily to get their golf fixes at one of the 18, 27
and 45-hole golf courses along Highway 8. And Calgary parents send their children to schools and riding
academies there on a daily basis. Shared communities of interests run both ways.

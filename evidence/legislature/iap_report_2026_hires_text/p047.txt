Rocky Mountain House-Sundre

This is one of two electoral divisions that the Chairman of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission,
in his Addendum to the Majority Report, recommended be restored. The other was Lac Ste. Anne.

In their Interim Report, the Commission proposed to remove the current Rimbey-Rocky Mountain House-
Sundre electoral division and replace it with a much larger electoral division comprised of portions of
the deleted electoral division and portions of Lacombe County including Gull Lake, Bentley, Lacombe,
Blackfalds, Clive and Alix, stretching east to Buffalo Lake, but not including Rimbey or Sundre. The
recommended replacement was an elongated east-west electoral division, as opposed to one simply
bordering the Eastern Slopes.

The problem facing the Commission was that population increases in Calgary and Edmonton and other
urban municipalities were deemed to require four new urban ridings, which meant removing two rural
electoral divisions. Prior to the Commission commencing its review, the Electoral Boundaries Commissions
Act was amended to add only two additional electoral divisions (87 to 89). The Commission considered that
the two additional electoral divisions would have to go to Edmonton and Calgary, one to each city. But since
Calgary's population had grown even more dramatically than Edmonton's population, the Commission also
recommended a second additional electoral division for Calgary.

In addition to recommending two additional seats for Calgary and one for Edmonton, the Commission was
also of the view that population changes in Airdrie and Cochrane also required that those two cities share
three electoral divisions instead of two.

The consequence of the Commission's recommendations to add effectively four seats to the cities was
the need to reduce the number of seats in rural Alberta by two. The Commission had only a finite number
of seats to map (89). And so, the Commission had to delete two rural seats to permit the four new urban
ones. The Commission recommended removing Rimbey-Rocky Mountain House-Sundre and Lac Ste. Anne.
However, the Commission did so reluctantly and expressed a concern about the cascading effects of having
to create several enlarged electoral divisions to cover the areas once covered by the two electoral divisions
which were eliminated.

Following the tabling of the Commission's Final Report replacing the Rimbey-Rocky Mountain House-Sundre
electoral division with the Lacombe-Clearwater electoral division, the Mayors of Lacombe and Blackfalds
expressed displeasure at having their municipalities be a part of a huge electoral division with Rocky
Mountain House and Nordegg.

The Chairman of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission was very much aware of this
dissatisfaction. In his Addendum to the Majority Report, Justice Miller pointed out that much of the objection
to the Commission's Interim Report was based on the loss of the two rural seats. He expressly lamented
those losses. He went on to state that if the Commission had been given 91 electoral divisions, there would
have been no loss of any seats outside of Edmonton and Calgary; although he conceded that his majority
colleagues did not agree with him on this.

Regardless, the Chairman then formulated an alternative recommendation in order to dissuade the
Legislature from accepting the Minority Report of the Commission's dissenting members. And that
recommendation was Recommendation 5. Recommendation 5 was the Chairman's recommendation and
his alone. For reasons which were not explained, the other members of his majority did not concur.

The recommendation was that if the Legislative Assembly found itself unable to concur in the
recommendations of the majority to remove the two electoral divisions, it should amend the Electoral
Divisions Act to increase the number of electoral divisions to 91 and restore the two electoral divisions that
the Commission reluctantly recommended removing.

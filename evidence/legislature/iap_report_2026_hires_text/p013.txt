3. Electoral Division Proposals

Lac Ste. Anne

Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland, as the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission named it in its Interim Report,
was one of the two electoral divisions that the Chairman of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission
recommended be "restored".

Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland, of course, is an existing electoral division under the Alberta Electoral Divisions Act.
The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission proposed its removal, but did so with regret.

The Alberta Legislature responded to that removal by urging the Government to introduce a Bill to amend the
Electoral Boundaries Commission Act to divide the Province into 91 proposed electoral divisions instead of
the current 89. The Electoral Divisions Act will require amending as well because, of course, it only provides
for 87 electoral divisions. The addition of the two electoral divisions would permit the restoration of Lac Ste.
Anne-Parkland.

In addition to urging the Government to increase the number of electoral divisions, the Legislature established
a Select Special Committee of the Legislature to oversee a review of Alberta's electoral boundaries
and directed the Committee to engage an advisory panel to incorporate, subject to modifications, the
recommendations made by the Chairman of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission. Those
recommendations were contained in the Chairman's Addendum to the Majority Report of the Commission.

In Recommendation 5, the Chairman of the Electoral Boundaries Commission recommended the restoration
of the two electoral divisions which the Commission removed. Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland was one of them.
The other was Rimbey-Rocky Mountain-Sundre.

The advisory panel has now reviewed the boundaries of the current (2017) Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland electoral
division. In conducting that review, the panel also examined the Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland electoral division
initially proposed by the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission in its Interim Report. As a result of that
review, the advisory panel is recommending that the current (2017) electoral division be roughly maintained in
its prior configuration.

That said, the advisory panel is recommending several changes to the current Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland boundaries.

The first and most significant change the panel is recommending is the incorporation of Stony Plain into the
Lac Ste. Anne electoral division. Stony Plain is immediately adjacent to the Lac Ste. Anne electoral division
and so the incorporation is easily accomplished. Under the current electoral map, Stony Plain is in an
electoral division known as Spruce Grove-Stony Plain.

The Lac Ste. Anne electoral division which the panel is recommending would run from Stony Plain to Onoway
to Mayerthorpe along a Highway 43 alignment. Highway 43 is the main highway which runs northwest from
Edmonton to Whitecourt, Fox Creek, Valleyview and Grande Prairie and is actually part of the CANAMEX
trilateral trade and transportation route connecting Canada, the United States and Mexico.

The Lac Ste. Anne electoral division that the panel recommends, like the current Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland,
would dip south of the Yellowhead Highway to take in Lake Wabamun and the Paul First Nation Reserve
along the east shore of Lake Wabamun, putting it in an electoral division with the First Nations in Lac Ste.
Anne, namely the Alexis Nakota Sioux Reserve at Lac Ste. Anne and the Alexander Cree Reserve northwest of
Edmonton. This reunites the three Treaty 6 Reserves in the current (2017) Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland, that were
divided by the Commission's recommendation.

12.The Law Governing Electoral Boundaries

The majority of the advisory panel based their electoral boundary proposals on the following understanding
of the law:

     1. Mapping of electoral divisions is subject to the Charter and therefore to judicial scrutiny.

     2. The Charter right is the right to effective representation. Departures will be found to violate
           the Charter.

     3. Voter parity or relative voter parity is the most important consideration in determining electoral
           division boundaries. (It is also the only empirically measurable consideration, although it does not
           prevent gerrymandering.)

     4. Deviations from voter parity may be justified on the grounds of practical impossibility or to provide
           more effective representation.

     5. Minority representation may justify deviation from voter parity to ensure that Legislatures represent
           the diversity of our populations. Voter parity may also be departed from in order to ensure effective
           representation to large, remote and sparsely-settled regions.

     6. Whether the Charter guarantee of effective representation extends to the "process" of electoral
           boundary making remains an open question. For example, whether or not a Legislature is required
           to establish a truly independent electoral boundaries commission with experts and the power to
           actually determine electoral boundaries, rather than simply recommend them to the Legislature,
           remains an open question. The recent Quebec Supreme Court case was argued on the basis that
           the alleged "interference" with the electoral boundaries determination process by the Quebec
           Legislature infringed the Charter. That is, the Supreme Court simply assumed a Charter breach
           and then went on to consider whether it was justified. The Supreme Court did not opine on whether
           there was, in fact or in law, a Charter breach. The constitutionality of the alleged act of interference
           by the Quebec Legislature with the Quebec electoral boundaries commission's process was
           not argued. Nevertheless, there have been non-binding judicial pronouncements on the issue of
           whether or not the electoral boundaries determination process itself is subject to Charter scrutiny.

           (a) Sopinka, J in the "Carter" case [1991] 2 SCR 158

                   "The Charter guarantee in S.3 does not extend to the process. The Legislature was not
                   required to establish an electoral commission or to ensure that a commission, when
                   established, was able to fulfill its mandate freely without guidelines imposed by the
                   Legislature." (obiter dicta in a dissenting opinion on another issue)

           (b) Coté and Rowe, J.J. in Quebec (A.G.) v. Lalande 2026 SCC 13

                   "In our opinion, while the Quebec legislature made the choice to delegate decision-
                   making authority for establishing the provincial electoral map to the CRÉ, making
                   provision for electoral representation ultimately falls within the legislation jurisdiction
                   of the Parliament of Quebec under S.45 of the Constitution Act, 1982. Moreover, even
                   when the provincial legislature has delegated this function, its involvement in adjusting
                   boundaries does not make the process unfair or arbitrary (Reference re Electoral
                   Boundaries at p 194)." (again, obiter dicta in a dissent on another issue)

Suffice it to say that if the electoral boundary determination process is completely delegated to an
independent commission, it will be done by an Act of the Legislature, an Act which is capable of amendment
or repeal. And so it should be. The Legislature is the body which is ultimately responsible for ensuring
effective representation. It is also the body which is most profoundly affected by an electoral boundaries
commission's proposals.

132. While preserving a purely Lethbridge seat guarantees a voice for the city, there will be three other MLAs
     whose electoral divisions overwhelmingly consist of non-city dwellers. These MLAs will not need the
     votes of Lethbridge residents to win re-election and the overall voice of Lethbridge will be unquestionably
     diluted. While there may nominally be four Lethbridge MLAs, there will only be one MLA whose job is
     truly to advocate for Lethbridge.

133. Our colleagues further suggest that this redraw is necessary because more significant hybridization will
     need to occur in Lethbridge at the next redistribution and it is prudent to draw electoral divisions that will
     be durable. This downplays the fact that they are prematurely dismantling the structure of the past 55
     years. More importantly, however, their solutions to the problem of the next redistribution are premature
     and speculative. There is no need to thrust a highly disruptive, unnecessary map onto Lethbridge when
     the present numbers do not require it and the next redistricting cycle will be able to approach this issue
     with more evidence and submissions.

134. Finally, we must respectfully observe that this map of the Lethbridge area bears all the hallmarks of a
     practice called "packing". Packing means that the electoral boundaries of a city and its surrounding area
     are drawn to make one electoral division contain most of the voters of one party (normally the opposition
     party). By creating a packed electoral division which will have an overwhelming number of voters for the
     opposition party, the rest of the electoral divisions in the city and surrounding areas will have fewer
     opposition voters. This makes it easier for the party who drew the boundaries to win additional seats. As
     noted above, we accept that our colleagues genuinely believe that they are improving effective
     representation. However, in light of the ABEBC Minority Report, and reaction thereto, from earlier this
     year, we are deeply concerned about whether the benefit of the doubt will be given to our colleagues. The
     impact on Alberta democracy and the trust the public has in our democratic institutions will be grave.

135. In sum, our colleagues' decision to hybridize Lethbridge is based on the premise that there are actually
     stronger communities of interest between those living in Lethbridge and areas two hours away via car,
     than between communities within Lethbridge itself. They conclude that the ABEBC erroneously
     concluded otherwise based on no public consultation and no new evidence. In fact, they conclude that
     their redraw of Lethbridge is "necessary" for effective representation. This is not a credible conclusion.

E. The Changes to Canmore-Banff and Okotoks-Diamond Valley Undermine Effective
    Representation for Both Electoral Divisions

136. Partially as a cascading consequence of the approach to Lethbridge we warn against above, our colleagues
     have been forced to place Diamond Valley into an electoral division with Banff and Canmore. The
     justification is that Diamond Valley is part of a "tourism corridor". The core nature of tourism in the
     Banff-Canmore corridor cannot be overstated. The Town of Banff estimates that 61% of all employment
     and 90% of the economy of Banff overall is linked to the tourism industry. It has an estimated capacity
     of approximately 3,700 hotel rooms. The Town of Canmore Labour Market Recruitment and Retention
     Strategy estimated that 33% of all employment in Canmore is linked to tourism.

Leduc-Beaumont

In his Addendum to the Majority Report, the Chairman of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission,
and he alone, suggest that an all-party Committee of the Legislature might wish to study the idea of restoring
Leduc-Beaumont, an electoral division which has been in place since 2010.

In accordance with its 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission Chair's suggestion, the advisory
panel has studied and considered the suggestion that Leduc-Beaumont be restored and we unanimously
recommend it. It makes sense to put Leduc and Beaumont in a single division.

Residents of Leduc and Beaumont, as well as their mayors, asked for this electoral division. The negative
reaction by people in Beaumont to being paired with Sherwood Park, which was recommended in the
2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission's Interim Report, caused Commission Chairman Miller and
our advisory panel to consider whether pairing Beaumont with Leduc might be more acceptable. This
was a situation in which residents had asked to be put in an electoral division with a significantly higher
than average population. That request lends some legitimacy to the advisory panel's questioning of the
importance of population parity as a consideration in achieving effective representation when people are
prepared to forego such parity in favour of being in an electoral division that suits their needs.

But it is important to understand the mapping of this electoral division because there are lessons in it for
those who wish to understand the electoral boundaries-making process.

The 2010 Electoral Boundaries Commission put Leduc and Beaumont together with Nisku. Likewise,
the 2017 Electoral Boundaries Commission mapped Leduc and Beaumont together. The 2025-2026
Electoral Boundaries Commission, in its Interim Report, proposed a Leduc-Beaumont electoral division
but with only the western half of Beaumont in it. The eastern half was put in an electoral division with
Strathcona-Sherwood Park. No reason was given for the decision to move the eastern half of Beaumont to
Strathcona-Sherwood Park, although it was likely due to the population growth of the two cities. But because
half of Beaumont was moved to Sherwood Park, the remaining Leduc-Beaumont electoral division actually
had to be enlarged to increase its population.

Then, in its Final Report, the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission recommended a completely
different configuration. It put Leduc and Beaumont in separate electoral divisions. The City of Beaumont,
in its entirety, was put in an Edmonton electoral division. The Commission called the Edmonton-Beaumont
electoral division "the most significant hybrid electoral division" it proposed. What made it "the most
significant" was not explained.

Notwithstanding that there was more support for Beaumont to be united with Leduc than to be united with
Edmonton, the Commission said that such an electoral division would not only have had an undesirably high
population (in excess of 68,000 people in the two cities alone) but it would have had "significant cascading
consequences on the rest of the Province which would have impeded effective representation." No
explanation was given as to what cascading consequences were being referred to, let alone how they could
affect the rest of the Province.

As stated above, in his Addendum to the Majority Report, the Chairman of the Commission, and he
alone, appeared to have had second thoughts about the Commission's recommendation to separate
Leduc and Beaumont because he suggested that an all-party Committee of the Legislature might wish
to study restoring the Leduc-Beaumont electoral division. The restoration of Leduc-Beaumont was not a
condition of the Chairman's recommendation to restore the two deleted rural electoral divisions as two
of our panel members suggest. Its connection to the restorations was not explained. The Chairman's
suggestion was simply that a Committee of the Legislature study the matter. The advisory panel has

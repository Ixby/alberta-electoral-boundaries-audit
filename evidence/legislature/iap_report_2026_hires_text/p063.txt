The 2010 Commission kept Whitehorn, Temple, Rundle and Pineridge together. It shifted the western
boundary from 36 Street to Deerfoot Trail, adding commercial lands, but did not cross the Trans-Canada
Highway to the south.

The 2017 Commission split the cluster: Whitehorn and Temple went to Calgary-Falconridge, while Rundle
and Pineridge remained in Calgary-Cross with Monterey Park. Calgary-Cross also crossed the Trans-Canada
Highway to include Marlborough and Marlborough Park. The issue now is whether those two communities
should remain in Calgary-Cross or return to a more east-oriented division.

The 2026 Commission's interim map reunited the four Properties neighbourhoods in Calgary-Cross,
included southern Monterey Park and extended east through Rocky View County toward Chestermere.
The final report moved much of the Rocky View territory north of the Trans-Canada Highway to Calgary-
Falconridge and added northern Monterey Park to Calgary-Cross. This sequence underscores the need for
a clearer framework: keep stable communities together, manage edge growth deliberately and use rural or
exurban additions only when they serve a coherent boundary purpose.

Why a pre-Commission secretariat is needed

The northeast Calgary challenge reached the panel late because no standing process tested growth
scenarios before the Commission began. A small working secretariat could address that gap without
displacing the Commission. It would assemble evidence, identify pressure points, develop transparent map
scenarios and show where the statutory population band, growth data and communities of interest create
genuine choices.

The secretariat should be time-limited and accountable. Beginning one year before the next boundary
commission, it would provide the Speaker with population estimates, municipal growth plans, prior
boundary decisions, community-of-interest notes and alternative maps. At minimum, it should prepare
five scenarios: a status quo baseline, a strict-average map, a growth-weighted map, a frozen-core map
protecting stable urban communities and an "after-next" map showing likely pressure one cycle later.

Its purpose would be to ensure the next Commission begins with tested options rather than discovering
growth dilemmas under deadline pressure.

For northeast Calgary, this process would have identified early that drawing every new division near the
average left too little capacity for known growth. It could also have presented the four-division map as a
tested option rather than a late-stage repair. The model would improve deliberation without limiting the
future Commission's judgment.

Recommended discussion position

The panel should recommend the four-division northeast Calgary plan: Calgary-Klein at about 62,300,
Calgary-Cross at about 61,800, Calgary-East at about 58,300 and Calgary-Confluence at about 51,000.
It should also recommend a pre-Commission secretariat for the next cycle to prepare neutral growth and
community-of-interest scenarios before the Commission begins its work.

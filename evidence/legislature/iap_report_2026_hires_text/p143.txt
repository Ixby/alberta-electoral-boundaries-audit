2) Mid-Size Cities Too Large for a Single Electoral Division, But Too Small for Two Electoral Divisions

94. The next group of electoral divisions that form "hybrids" are those that have populations that, in a single
     electoral division, would either exceed the 25% threshold from the average population of an electoral
     division, or would be so great that it is obviously unfair to have the population of those communities in a
     single electoral division. However, such cities are too small to form two electoral divisions on their own.
     The following table has the list of these electoral divisions (where we are agreed), the main city in their
     electoral division, and the 2024 population of the main community as listed by the Alberta Municipal
     Affairs official population estimates4:

Table 2. Hybrids with Mid-Size Cities

Airdrie-East                Airdrie                                               85,505
Airdrie-West                Airdrie                                               85,505
Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche  Fort McMurray5                                        83,467

95. The population average for a 91 electoral division provincial electoral boundary map is 53,722 with a
     permissible range of +/- 25% or 40,292 to 67,153. As one can see, all of the above cities with the exception
     of Medicine Hat would be illegal to put in a single electoral division, and a Medicine Hat electoral division
     would be the most populous of all the electoral divisions proposed in the ABEBC majority report.
     Dividing any of these urban centres into two electoral divisions would either be smaller than the legally
     permissible size or make them right on the threshold of legality.

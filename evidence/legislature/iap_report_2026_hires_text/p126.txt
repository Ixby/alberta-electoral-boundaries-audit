Minority Opinion - Panel of the Special Select Committee on Electoral Boundaries           7

IV) How to Restore the Two Rural Electoral Divisions

A) Lac Ste. Anne

21. One of the two rural electoral divisions removed by the Majority Report, which Justice Miller suggested
     could be restored, is Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland. The Panel unanimously proposes a successor electoral
     division of "Lac Ste. Anne", mostly consisting of:

a) the Town of Stony Plain;
b) all of Lac Ste. Anne County;
c) the three Treaty 6 reserves previously in Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland; and
d) the most northern portions of Parkland County as demonstrated on the IAP Majority map.

22. This is a coherent electoral division west and north of Edmonton, bringing together large parts of Treaty
     6 territory. There are clear transportation routes that run throughout it travelling northwest of Edmonton.
     The electoral division has a reasonable population. Three Treaty 6 reserves that the ABEBC Majority
     Report divided are reunited. Effective representation will be achieved.

23. As a result of this restoration of Lac Ste. Anne, Barrhead-Westlock-Athabasca can return to borders much
     closer to the status quo, though exchanging some territory with Cold Lake-Bonnyville-St. Paul for reasons
     outlined in the Interim Report. It has lost territory to Lesser Slave Lake, in recognition of MLA van
     Dijken's concern about representing too many counties. The Panel does not propose changing Lesser
     Slave Lake back to its current borders, consistent with Justice Miller's instructions to preserve the ABEBC
     Majority Map to the extent possible. Barrhead-Westlock-Athabasca has accordingly become smaller and
     more compact. This will increase effective representation, and mitigates the MLA's concerns with
     representing too many municipalities across too vast a geographic distance.

24. Because of these changes, Smoky Lake County can leave Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche. This means that
     the electoral divisions of Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche and Fort McMurray-Wood Buffalo can return to
     the same borders as the 2017 redistribution and as outlined in the ABEBC Interim Report. This restoration
     of the status quo reduces the population in these vast rural areas. This increases effective representation for
     these areas and minimizes disruptions to the status quo. We are not certain the IAP Majority agrees with
     restoring the boundaries of Fort McMurray-Wood Buffalo.

25. Another result of the recreation of the Lac Ste. Anne electoral division is the reduction in the size of St.
     Albert-Sturgeon, by keeping Alexander First Nation in Lac Ste. Anne. This will facilitate effective
     representation. The electoral division now includes the northeastern corner of St. Albert and the other
     parts of Sturgeon County outside Alexander First Nation. The Panel unanimously proposes that this
     electoral division be renamed Morinville-Sturgeon. The low population of Morinville-Sturgeon is justified
     by the high population growth in this area. This population growth is already significant and the possible
     development of Meta's artificial intelligence data centre will likely increase that. Despite the gap in
     population, we do not propose readjusting the border with St. Albert. The imbalance in population with
     St. Albert is justified to keep as much of the City of St. Albert in a single electoral division as possible (in
     line with several submissions), and to follow Justice Miller's instructions to preserve the ABEBC Majority
     Report's map to the extent possible.

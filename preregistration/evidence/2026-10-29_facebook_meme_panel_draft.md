- **Why it is kept:** it is the only information about the panel's draft that the audit
  had before the three added tests were signed (2026-10-29 18:20 MDT; now Amendment 5, Part 17)
  and before set D was made primary (Amendment 5, Parts 14 and 18). The entry that first signed
  the tests called it "public reporting" (wording not carried into Amendment 5). Two of those
  tests (Amendment 5, Part 17) address claims made here:
  - the **town-split test** (Lethbridge split into four ridings; Okotoks split);
  - the **city-versus-rest test** (Calgary and Edmonton ridings averaging 7,000 more
    residents per MLA, a 14% difference).
  
  Both tests were fixed before any panel or committee map was published or seen (the panel
  reported to the committee on October 22; its report was not public), and they apply the same
  way to whatever the published map contains.

## Transcription

> Some pretty wild takeaways from today's electoral boundaries committee meeting:
>
> Lethbridge would be split into FOUR ridings instead of its current two urban ones. Three
> would combine city neighbourhoods with surrounding rural areas.
>
> Three urban-rural hybrid ridings are proposed along Calgary's northwest and western edges.
> Springbank gets split up. Kathleen Ganley questioned why the panel was citing GOLF COURSES
> to justify its boundaries.
>
> Okotoks and Diamond Valley would be split apart, with Diamond Valley paired with Banff. The
> panel's dissenting members say public submissions overwhelmingly favoured keeping them
> together.
>
> Ganley says Calgary and Edmonton ridings would average 7,000 more residents per MLA than
> ridings outside those cities. That's a 14 percent difference.
>
> The NDP wanted to put its concerns about the maps on the record. UCP MLAs voted 3–2 to end
> the committee's review before those motions could even be considered.
>
> The NDP also wanted taxpayers to know how much the panel and its outside consultants were
> paid. UCP MLAs voted that down too. Ganley says the panel chair gets an extra $5,000/month
> on top of regular pay.
>
> The UCP says the maps should be debated in the Legislature once they're public. The NDP
> wanted the committee to scrutinize them now.

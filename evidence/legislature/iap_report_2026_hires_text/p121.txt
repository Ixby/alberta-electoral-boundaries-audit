Minority Opinion - Panel of the Special Select Committee on Electoral Boundaries  2

Departing from a narrow interpretation of the Panel's mandate and Justice Miller's prescribed non-partisan
terms is not a minor or technical issue of legal interpretation. Setting aside constitutionality, it may be considered
an inappropriate use of the delegated authority under Motion 37 that our colleagues' advice is given at all. By
unreasonably interpreting this Panel's mandate, and thus giving this inappropriate advice, our
colleagues imperil the constitutional ability of the legislature to rely on this Panel's advice. The
legislature must forbear and consider only the advice to restore the two rural electoral divisions in a
constitutionally compliant manner.

We respond to the substance of our colleagues' proposed additional changes to areas south of Airdrie as a
matter of thoroughness, even though it is our position that these recommendations are beyond this Panel's
mandate.

It is our view that our colleagues' recommendations to move the communities of Springbank and Bearspaw
into three Calgary electoral divisions are unnecessary to achieve effective representation. They also sit
uncomfortably with Supreme Court of Canada and Alberta Court of Appeal decisions on voter parity and
hybrid electoral divisions. Changes that our colleagues have recommended to four electoral divisions in
Northeast and East Calgary also seem unnecessary to achieve effective representation.

Our colleagues have recommended removing an urban seat from Lethbridge, and replacing it with a mostly
rural electoral division. In other words, they have taken this Panel's mandate to add two rural electoral
divisions to the map and have added three. This has significant cascading impacts throughout
Southern Alberta that, in our respectful opinion, actively undermine effective representation.

In general, the changes that our colleagues have proposed to electoral divisions south of Airdrie are based on
two premises. One is that hybrid electoral divisions ought to be used more and that their use is justified even if
it is not required to provide sensible electoral divisions with populations largely approximating the provincial
average. The other is that the concept of "community of interest" can be used to justify incorporating rural
regions with urban communities, even if the urban communities already have sensible electoral boundaries with
populations that largely approximate the provincial average. These premises are highly contestable as a matter
of good governance and best redistricting practices. Most of our colleagues' proposed boundaries were also
clearly opposed by the vast majority of submissions that the ABEBC received. While we take it that our
colleagues genuinely believe that this is in the best interests of creating the most effective representation for
Alberta, the consequence is partisan outcomes that they either ignore or favour.

Our minority report speaks to legislators: any adoption of recommendations beyond those tied directly to
the restoration of the two rural divisions on the terms of Recommendation 5 will be constitutionally
fraught. The courts are likely to become involved. A real possibility is that the courts end up imposing a map
for the next provincial election. If so, the elimination of the two rural electoral divisions may well be ordered.
This will be a most unfortunate outcome for Alberta democracy. Our full report expands upon why.

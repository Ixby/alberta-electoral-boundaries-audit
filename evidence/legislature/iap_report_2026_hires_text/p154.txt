138. Moreover, there was overwhelming desire for Okotoks and Diamond Valley to be paired in the same
     electoral division after the ABEBC Interim Report. Not a single submission, to our knowledge, asked for
     Diamond Valley to be paired with Banff. Okotoks and especially Diamond Valley are small, agriculture
     and commuter-based municipalities outside Calgary in Foothills County. They are very different from a
     cultural and economic perspective from the mountain, tourism-based communities of Banff and Canmore.
     Diamond Valley shares the Sheep River watershed with Okotoks. Diamond Valley and Okotoks have
     shared municipal services in waste and recreation. The two communities are very closely tied together.
     Separating them to add population to the Canmore-Banff electoral division makes little sense.

139. One area of Foothills County is more tied to the Canmore-Banff electoral division. The Eden Valley
     Reserve is a Stoney Nakoda Bearspaw Nation reserve more culturally tied to the Bearspaw Nation Mini
     Thni Reserve. Accordingly, it was also included in the ABEBC Canmore-Banff electoral division. The
     Eden Valley Reserve is clearly distinguishable, in terms of its links to Canmore-Banff, from Diamond
     Valley.

140. While we have some sympathy for our colleagues' view that Canmore-Banff is an odd candidate for s 15(2)
     status given the wealth of the electoral division and the proximity to Calgary, the ABEBC addressed this
     issue comprehensively in the Majority Report:

           The Commission has elected to give the electoral division s. 15(2) status given the high
           Indigenous population, distance from the Legislature, and the two towns at its heart not neatly
           fitting in with adjacent areas from a communities of interest perspective. The residents, even
           those located outside the national park, are largely industry dependent on Banff National Park,
           giving the electoral division a common interest. Multiple reserves are also in this electoral
           division.

           We recognize that Jasper also fits in with this electoral division for these reasons. That is why it
           was included in this electoral division in the interim report. However, for reasons noted above,
           the Commission is persuaded that the logistics of Banff and Jasper being in the same electoral
           division are not feasible. Jasper's residents receive their services from the provincial government
           in Hinton or Edmonton, not Banff or Canmore. Moreover, Jasper and Banff are separated by
           hundreds of kilometres with no inhabitants between them (apart from the modest population
           in Lake Louise), and a road that is impassable for several months of the year. Accordingly, the
           Commission declines to place Jasper in this electoral division, but, in recognition of the
           communities of interest, gives the electoral division s. 15(2) status. Effective representation will
           follow.

Banff-Canmore-Diamond Valley

The current Banff-Kananaskis electoral division was once a Banff-Cochrane electoral division until Cochrane
was moved out by the 2017 Electoral Boundaries Commission. The 2017 Commission proposed an electoral
division combining Banff National Park with Canmore, Kananaskis Country, the Stoney and Tsuut'ina First
Nations Reserves and Springbank into a rather large electoral division. Cochrane was put in an electoral
division with West Airdrie. That gave Cochrane an urban voice undiluted by the unique concerns of the
mountain park communities.

The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission proposed a similar-sized electoral division with similar
boundaries, although the Commission proposed moving the Tsuut'ina First Nation Reserve into the Calgary
riding of Calgary-Glenmore and Springbank into a Cochrane-Springbank electoral division.

As a consequence of the restoration of the Rocky Mountain House-Sundre electoral division, Banff-
Kananaskis lost a lot of territory in its northeast quadrant. Also, as a consequence of the Commission's
recommendation to put Springbank into an electoral division with Cochrane, the proposed Canmore-Banff
electoral division lost a lot of population. It would also continue to be without the Springbank population if
the advisory panel's proposal to put Springbank into Calgary-Bow and Calgary-West is approved.

The Commission's response to the population loss was to recommend making Banff a special consideration
electoral division pursuant to Section 15(2) of the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act. But even as the
Commission mapped it, the electoral division was only 27.2% below the provincial average, almost but not
quite within the permitted variance of plus or minus 25%.

Furthermore, members of advisory panel were of the view that the intent of the Legislature in providing
for electoral divisions which may have a population as much as 50% below the average population was
to provide effective representation to people in huge, sparsely settled areas of the Province far away from
Edmonton. It was not intended for an electoral division with two large upscale and prosperous towns on the
TransCanada Highway, an hour out of Calgary, with air and bus service to Edmonton. Banff, at roughly 10,000
population, and Canmore, at roughly 17,000 population and growing, are not sparsely settled communities.
They are not even far apart and the nearby Stoney First Nation at Morley adds another 5,000 people.

So the advisory panel is recommending against conferring Section 15(2) status on these two high-end
vacation home communities or any electoral division which contains them.

But the Act does mandate that the population of ordinary electoral divisions must not be more than
25% below the average population. The addition of Diamond Valley and nearby hamlets or villages adds
population of this electoral division.

So the advisory panel is proposing what really amounts to a minor change to the Canmore-Banff electoral
division proposed by the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission to capture the necessary population.
The change is to the electoral division's southwest boundary to take in not only the Bearspaw First Nation
Reserve near Longview, but also the Highway 22 corridor north through Longview, Diamond Valley, Millarville,
Priddis and Bragg Creek. Other than this change and the adjustment to the northeast boundary of the
electoral division to accommodate the restoration of the Rocky Mountain House-Sundre electoral division,
Banff-Canmore-Diamond Valley is similar to what the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission
recommended, except that it does not require S.15(2) status.

The advisory panel recommends adding the Longview-Diamond Valley-Millarville-Priddis-Bragg Creek
corridor to Banff-Canmore to avoid having to rely on Section 15(2) and to keep the Bow Valley, Kananaskis
Country and the Foothills south and west of Calgary together. Diamond Valley, Millarville and Priddis and
Bragg Creek all supply population to the electoral district in the same way that Springbank once did, and
the connection of Diamond Valley, Millarville, Priddis and Bragg Creek to Kananaskis and Banff is arguably
stronger than that the connection of Springbank to Banff.

Minority Opinion - Panel of the Special Select Committee on Electoral Boundaries                  27

102. Restoring the two rural electoral divisions can be practically done without diluting Calgarians' voter parity.
     The "practical impossibility" exception to a requirement of voter parity is accordingly inapplicable. As
     such, the only way these changes can be justified is if they provide more effective representation.

2) The Changes Are Not Necessary to Achieve Effective Representation

103. We have no doubt that there are residents of Bearspaw and Springbank that consider themselves part of
     a community of interest that includes parts of Calgary. Several Springbank residents wrote to that effect.
     If one is on either side of the border between Calgary and these communities, one does not necessarily
     know it, especially vis-à-vis Bearspaw Village. Residents of these communities receive many services in
     Calgary and travel along the same roads. Indeed, one of us (Kennedy) probably would have agreed to place
     these communities in Calgary electoral divisions in a map of 89 electoral divisions to soften the blow of
     the loss of rural electoral divisions.

104. However, we also have no doubt that many residents of Bearspaw and Springbank have consciously
     chosen not to live in Calgary, and desire that to be reflected in their representation. And in these particular
     circumstances, these changes should only be recommended if effective representation required that
     Springbank and Bearspaw be in Calgary electoral divisions. In our view, this is hardly a requirement.
     Rebecca Brown was the only resident of Bearspaw who wrote about this issue:

I live in Bearspaw, a rural area just outside Calgary. I am participating because I believe that
rural and urban boundaries should remain distinct and that rural areas should remain
connected to rural communities rather than being absorbed into urban ones. I similarly
believe that urban boundaries should remain urban, to reflect the population density and their
differing needs. Rural areas are unique, they contain tight-knit communities where neighbors
know each other and share a lifestyle that is fundamentally different from the urban experience.
Our priorities, such as agricultural land use, rural infrastructure, are very distinct from
those of a city, or large town. Combining rural ridings with large cities such as Calgary,
for example, will dilute our voice and make it harder to advocate for the needs that
matter most to us. Urban areas face challenges like transit expansion and dense housing, while,
for example, Bearspaw's concerns center more on keeping large industrial and retail from
encroaching on too much of the land, ensuring density doesn't impact our infrastructure
and protecting our water and lands from contamination. Keeping rural areas like
Bearspaw aligned with other rural areas ensures effective representation and respects
the principle of those communities. [Emphasis added]

105. Ms. Brown is, of course, speaking only for herself. But holding that it is "necessary" to disregard this
     perspective and others like it in the name of effective representation strikes us as results-oriented and
     imbalanced. Bearspaw and Springbank remain in Rocky View County. Unlike Elbow Valley, which is
     integrated into Calgary's wastewater system (warranting the differential treatment between Elbow Valley
     and Bearspaw and Springbank), these communities receive few if any municipal services from Calgary.
     The residents of these areas pay significantly less property tax as a result of their living in Rocky View
     County rather than Calgary. The population density, income ranges and typical transportation options
     available all reflect residents who have chosen a country residential lifestyle and range of services that is
     vastly different from the choices and everyday lifestyles of the residents of Calgary neighbourhoods.

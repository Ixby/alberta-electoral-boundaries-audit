Minority Opinion - Panel of the Special Select Committee on Electoral Boundaries  3

I) Introduction

1. In October 2025, the properly constituted and independent Alberta Electoral Boundaries Commission
     (the "ABEBC") submitted a unanimous interim report (the "Interim Report") to the Legislative Assembly
     of Alberta, recommending a redistribution of Alberta's electoral divisions from 87 to 89 for the next
     provincial general election. This complied with the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act, RSA 2000, c E-3
     (the "Act").

2. In March 2026, the final report revealed a sharply divided commission. The final majority report (the
     "ABEBC Majority Report") made relatively modest amendments to the Interim Report, trying to balance
     the exceptional population growth in the cities of Calgary, Edmonton, and Airdrie, while recognizing the
     particular representational challenges of rural and especially northern Alberta. The ABEBC Majority
     Report reversed a decades-long trend in Alberta redistricting towards greater population parity between
     electoral divisions. Even so, the need for an additional seat in Airdrie, a net of two in Calgary, and a net
     of one in Edmonton required the removal of two rural seats. The Minority Report ("ABEBC Minority
     Report") insisted that no rural seats could be removed. Though the motivation to preserve rural seats is
     laudable, the ABEBC Minority Report produced maps that were not compliant with case law regarding
     redistricting in Canada in general and Alberta in particular. These maps defied common sense in many
     cases and were not a reasonable exercise of the discretion given to the ABEBC under the Act. These were
     what Justice McLachlin (as she then was) warned about in Reference re Prov. Electoral Boundaries
     (Sask.), [1991] 2 S.C.R. 158 ("Carter") at p 189: "reasonable persons applying the appropriate principles
     . . . could not have set [these] electoral boundaries".

3. Justice Dallas K Miller, Chair of the ABEBC, was clearly deeply concerned that the Legislative Assembly
     of Alberta (the "legislature") may adopt the ABEBC Minority Report. Accordingly, he added an
     Addendum to the ABEBC Majority Report (the "Addendum"). He was the only one of the five
     commissioners to agree to the contents of the Addendum. He provided a "Recommendation 5" wherein
     he recommended that, if the legislature could not accept the ABEBC Majority Report because it eliminated
     two rural electoral divisions, the size of the legislature be expanded by an additional two seats to 91, such
     that no rural electoral divisions would need to be removed. Given the obvious partisan implications of the
     restoration of the two rural electoral divisions, he imposed strict conditions on their restoration to stem
     further boundary changes that may create partisan advantage, notably by prohibiting "impact on any
     electoral division in Airdrie or containing territory south of Airdrie's southernmost border".

4. The warnings from the ABEBC majority succeeded in dissuading the legislature from adopting the
     ABEBC Minority Report. The Legislature subsequently passed Motion 37, establishing a Select Special
     Committee on Electoral Boundaries (the "Committee"). This in turn created this Independent Advisory
     Panel (the "Panel" or the "IAP") to provide advice on how to set electoral boundaries that implement
     Recommendation 5, "subject to any modifications [the Panel] considers necessary, taking into
     consideration the requirement for effective representation as guaranteed by the Canadian Charter of Rights
     and Freedoms".

5. In our view, this irregular process to restore the two rural electoral divisions is constitutionally acceptable
     so long as we stay within our strict mandate of implementing Recommendation 5, subject to modifications
     necessary to implement Recommendation 5's purpose. It is commendable that the legislature created this
     Independent Advisory Panel to help ensure that politicians are not choosing their own voters.

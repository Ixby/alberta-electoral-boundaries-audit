# Lane 3 — Process Scorecard (DRAFT, not yet registered)

> **STATUS: DRAFT — NOT PRE-REGISTERED (created 2026-07-14).** This document
> proposes a third audit lane and is published for transparency before
> registration. It carries **no confirmatory weight** until the author files it
> as an OSF registration, which must happen **before the Lunty committee's
> report is released (due 2026-11-02)** for the November application to count
> as prospective. Applied to the 2025–26 commission process, the scorecard is
> **retrospective and descriptive only** — those facts were public before this
> document existed, and no claim of prediction attaches to them.

## Purpose

The audit's two existing lanes score the *maps*: Lane 2 measures geometry
(five structural tests) and Lane 1 measures vote-to-seat conversion (four
fairness metrics against the neutral ensemble). The norms dimension — the
subject of the site's third companion page — currently exists only as
narrative. This lane scores the *process* that produces a map, using the
protective features the comparative redistricting literature identifies and
the sixty-year Canadian practice embodies. The purpose is symmetry over
rhetoric: the same checklist, applied to every process this audit examines,
so the norms question ("did this process behave like the institution the
norm describes?") becomes a scored comparison instead of an accusation.

## Design rules

1. **Binary or ternary cells only.** Every criterion is answerable
   Yes / No / Partial from public documents. No judgment scales.
2. **Symmetric application.** The same criteria score the 2025–26 commission
   process, the Lunty committee process, and any future process the audit
   examines. Criteria were chosen from the literature and the statutory
   record, not from either process's known weaknesses — see the
   retrospective-bias disclosure below.
3. **Counting rule.** The lane's summary is a raw count of protective
   features present, exactly parallel to Lane 2's raw tally. It is not a
   calibrated probability and must never be presented as one.
4. **No verdict.** A low count documents departure from protective practice.
   It does not establish illegitimacy, bad faith, or unconstitutionality.

## The nine criteria

| # | Protective feature | Binary definition | Source of record |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Arm's-length drafting | The body that drafts the boundaries is institutionally independent of the parties that will contest them (advisory input to a partisan decider scores Partial) | EBCA; Motions 19/37 |
| P2 | Public hearings before adoption | Hearings open to the public are held before the boundaries are finalized | Commission final report; CBC reporting on committee procedure |
| P3 | Draft published for challenge | A complete draft map is published, with a comment period, before the final version is settled | Commission interim report; committee procedure as announced |
| P4 | New public submissions accepted | The process accepts and logs new written submissions from the public | Commission submission archive (1,147); Motion 37 terms |
| P5 | Demonstrated responsiveness | The record shows at least one material boundary change attributable to public input | Commission interim-to-final reversals (80+ submissions credited) |
| P6 | Neutral chair of the deciding body | The body whose vote settles the map is chaired by a person not affiliated with a contesting party | EBCA s.4 (Chief Justice names chair); Motion 19 (committee chaired by a government MLA) |
| P7 | Opposition presence in the deciding body | The deciding body includes members nominated by or affiliated with the opposition | EBCA (two opposition nominees); Motion 19 (two of five) |
| P8 | Published reasons for contested choices | Written rationales for contested boundary decisions are published with the map | Both commission reports; committee report pending |
| P9 | Precedent conformity | The process matches the form used in prior Canadian redistribution cycles reviewed by this audit | §5.9 comparative review; Bratt correspondence |

The commission process scores 9 of 9 *by construction* — the criteria describe
the institution the norm built, and the commission was that institution. The
informative comparison is prospective: what the committee process does with
its pending and absent features between now and November.

## Prospective application (the registrable test)

**Object:** the Lunty committee process, scored on the day its report is
released (due 2026-11-02), using public documents only.

**Predicted direction (to be locked at registration):** as constituted, the
committee process scores **No or Pending on P2, P3, P4, P5, P6, and P9**. The
committee can falsify the prediction on four of those six between now and
November — by holding hearings (P2), publishing a draft for challenge (P3),
opening new submissions (P4), and thereby enabling responsiveness (P5).

**Pass condition (mitigation):** if the released process scores Yes on P2 and
P3 and at least one of P4/P5, the audit's process-departure finding
(Casing 13) is materially mitigated and will be restated as such within the
same 72-hour window the map scorecard already commits to.

**Fail condition is not a verdict:** if the process scores as predicted, the
audit reports the count and the comparison with the commission's 9 of 9 —
and stops there, per the standing rule.

## Retrospective-bias disclosure

This scorecard is being drafted *after* the committee's structure was
announced, by an author who has already published findings critical of the
minority map and the April 16 process. The criteria were selected from the
protective features the comparative literature names and the EBCA embodies —
but a skeptic is entitled to note that any checklist drafted mid-episode
risks fitting the known facts. Three mitigations: (1) the criteria set is
frozen at OSF registration and cannot grow or shrink for November; (2) every
criterion also scores the commission process, so the instrument cannot be
tuned against one side without touching the other; (3) the committee holds
the falsification lever — the predicted No cells are all features it can
still add.

## What this lane is not

Lane 3 measures process features, not outcomes and not intent. A process can
score 9 of 9 and still produce a skewed map; a process can score low and
produce a fair one. That is why the lanes are kept separate and why the
November evaluation runs all three.

Minority Opinion - Panel of the Special Select Committee on Electoral Boundaries                                 26

B. The IAP Majority Inclusion of Springbank and Bearspaw in Calgary Electoral Divisions Is
    Not Necessary to Achieve Effective Representation and Risks Offending the Charter

99. Our colleagues' maps of Calgary are, unlike the ABEBC Minority maps of Calgary, a plausible set of
     boundaries that, from a substantive perspective, would normally achieve the constitutional minimum
     requirement of effective representation. However, the West Calgary maps subtly dilute Calgarians' votes
     in ways that are not necessary to achieve effective representation. This sits uncomfortably with Supreme
     Court of Canada and Alberta Court of Appeal precedent. Given that we are bound by such precedent,
     there is a real risk that the maps will be found unconstitutional, especially in the irregular circumstances in
     which this Panel's work is unfolding. We reach this conclusion somewhat reluctantly, because we are
     concerned about excessive constitutionalization of political matters. But we feel obliged to warn the
     legislature of the risks and perceived unfairness in this regard.

1) The IAP Majority's Maps Increase the Population of Calgary Electoral Divisions Despite the Reduction in Average
    Population of Electoral Divisions

100. By creating more hybrid electoral divisions in Calgary, the Panel Majority has increased the average
     population of Calgary electoral divisions. This is notwithstanding the fact that the average population per
     electoral division throughout the province has decreased as a result of the expansion of the legislature to
     91 electoral divisions. The ABEBC Majority report proposed Calgary electoral divisions with an average
     population of 56,379, or 2.64% above the provincial average of 54,929. The Panel Majority changes raise
     the average population of Calgary electoral divisions to 56,726, or 5.59% above the provincial average of
     53,722:

In order to incorporate these additional hybrid areas into Calgary, Calgary would need 1.5 more electoral
divisions to match the provincial average population. That matching is, of course, not required. But, as we
come to below, it is the most important factor in drawing electoral divisions. This inflation of the
population of Calgary electoral divisions, despite the decrease in the average population of all other
electoral divisions, requires justification.

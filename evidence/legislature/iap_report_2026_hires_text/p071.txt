The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission considered but chose not to include Springbank in
Calgary Bow because the Commission said it would make the population of the electoral division too large.
The information we were provided estimates the population of Calgary-Bow-Springbank at about 58,673 or
9% above the average population of all the electoral divisions in Alberta (53,722), well within the variance
permitted by Section 15 (1) of the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act.
It is also important to note that despite the Commission's usage of the word "hybrid" and "hybridization", a
Calgary Bow-Springbank electoral division, if approved, would not be a hybrid riding. True hybrids mix people
with fundamentally different interests. Hybrid ridings mix urban and rural (agrarian) residents in electoral
divisions. The proposed electoral division does little of that. It is simply an electoral division which straddles
or extends beyond the city limits into a high growth area where urban-like development has long since taken
place. It mixes urban with urban residents. It does not mix much rural with urban.
Communities of interest are shaped by transportation corridors, employment, shared services, shared
recreation facilities, golf courses, bike routes, soccer pitches, shared schools, etc. Municipal boundaries in
and of themselves have little to do with communities of interest in circumstances like those existing in west
Calgary and Springbank.
And as far as effective representation is concerned, the advisory panel is of the view that the communities
proposed to be conjoined for representation purposes are not fundamentally different, nor are their interests
conflicting or competing.
In conclusion, the majority of the advisory panel recommends that the Legislature consider mapping North
Springbank into the Calgary-Bow electoral division and naming it Calgary-Bow-Springbank.

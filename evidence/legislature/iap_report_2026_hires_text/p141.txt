86. Our colleagues rest their recommendations for different boundaries in West/Northwest Calgary and

    A. Hybrid Electoral Divisions

87. In their IAP Majority Report, our colleagues accurately describe what a blended or "hybrid" electoral
     division is. We also agree that it is a tool that can be used, when it would grant effective representation, to
     provide for situations when it will ease the representation of people in more sparsely populated areas. Our
     colleagues say that using that type of electoral division in southwest Alberta is justified based on the extent
     of the current use of that tool and in keeping with the way it is used in other parts of the province.
     However, those justifications misunderstand and misdescribe the current use of hybrid electoral divisions
     in the ABEBC's majority report. They also ignore the most obvious analog to Lethbridge in the province--
     Red Deer--where hybrid electoral divisions are not in use.

88. Our colleagues discuss the number of "hybrid" electoral divisions in the province. However, a closer
     review of those classifications essentially reveals that this single categorization includes three different
     scenarios:

     1. Minor areas that are added to large city areas because they bear more in common with the city than
           surrounding rural areas;

     2. Mid-size cities with a population that is too large to accommodate a single electoral division but too
           small to accommodate two electoral divisions; and

     3. Small and mid-size cities with a population that is too small to justify a single electoral division and
           are combined with other communities.

     In our view, these are three very different types of hybrids. While all can be justified on the basis of
     providing more effective representation, why they do so differs for each type of hybrid. For Category 1,
     the hybrid provides a better community of interest and therefore better representation. For Categories 2
     and 3, there is no choice but to use hybrids. Category 3 in particular obfuscates what hybridization tends
     to refer to when it has come up in legal proceedings and secondary literature. Our colleagues conflate these
     types of hybrids and the reasons for their use. This risks being misleading, especially because none of the
     three types of hybrids are analogous to Lethbridge.

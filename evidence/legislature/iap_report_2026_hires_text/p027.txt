Drayton Valley-Devon

Drayton Valley-Devon is another electoral division the panel is proposing be restored to roughly its
current configuration.

The proposed restoration of the Drayton Valley-Devon electoral division was made possible by the
restoration of the Lac Ste. Anne electoral division. Drayton Valley-Devon is located south of the Lac Ste. Anne
electoral division, and its northern boundary was therefore impacted by the recommended restoration of
Lac Ste. Anne. But, more importantly, the panel's review of the electoral divisions adjacent to Lac Ste. Anne
suggested opportunities for electoral boundary improvements and/or rationalization. In its Interim Report,
the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission mapped this electoral division as a variant of the current
Drayton Valley-Devon electoral division, which suggest to the advisory panel that, but for the deletion of
Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland, the Commission might have mapped Drayton Valley-Devon much as we are now
proposing. In its Final Report, the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission mapped a Stony Plain-
Drayton Valley electoral division. Devon, Thorsby, and Calmar were proposed to be put in a Leduc-Devon
electoral division. This was not considered to be ideal.

The current (2017) Drayton Valley-Devon electoral division runs from Devon, through Calmar, Thorsby,
Warburg, Alsike, Drayton Valley along Highway 39 and beyond to places like Lodgepole, Brazeau and
Cynthia. It also straddles the North Saskatchewan River from Devon to Drayton Valley.

In its Final Report the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission conceded that the relationship between
Stony Plain and Drayton Valley was not obvious. The Commission relied on the geographic proximity of
Stony Plain to Devon to recommend that they be put together in a Leduc-Devon electoral division. Arguably,
the geographic proximity of Stony Plain to Lac Ste. Anne is equally strong. But, more importantly, the
Commission's Interim Report recognized the desirability of preserving as much as possible the current
(2017) electoral division of Drayton Valley-Devon. We agree.

But with the elimination the Lac Ste. Anne electoral division, the Commission's Final Report changed the
Drayton Valley-Devon electoral division drastically. Devon, Calmar and Thorsby were moved to a new Leduc-
Devon electoral division. Lake Wabamun and surrounding communities, including the Paul First Nation
Reserve, were proposed to be part of a Stony Plain-Drayton Valley electoral division. The Paul First Nation
Reserve was thereby proposed to be in an electoral division separate and apart from the Alexis Nakoda Sioux
Nations' Reserve around Lac Ste. Anne and Alexander Cree First Nation Reserve northwest of Edmonton,
near the boundary between Lac Ste. Anne County and Sturgeon County.

The recommended restoration of a Lac Ste. Anne electoral division has given the advisory panel the
opportunity to restore a Drayton Valley-Devon electoral division.

The current Drayton Valley-Devon electoral boundary makes a right turn at the intersection of Highway 22
and Highway 16 and heads east for several kilometres before heading south again on Highway 759 to avoid
taking Lake Wabamun out of Lac Ste. Anne. We recommend that electoral boundary to keep Lake Wabamun
in Lac Ste. Anne. It also keeps both Entwistle and Evansburg in Lac Ste. Anne. The separation of those two
communities on either side of the Pembina River has been an issue before prior boundary commissions.

One final note, the eastern boundary of Drayton Valley-Devon is right next to the Edmonton International
Airport and the Edmonton-Nisku electoral division. Further south the eastern boundary of Drayton
Valley-Devon abuts the City of Leduc and the Leduc-Beaumont electoral division. There may need to be
adjustments made to the eastern boundary of Drayton Valley-Devon to accommodate Edmonton-Nisku and
Leduc-Beaumont.

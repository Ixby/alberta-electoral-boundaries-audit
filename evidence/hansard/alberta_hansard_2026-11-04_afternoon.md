**Where:** Oral Question Period, "Electoral Boundaries Committee Report", page 2090.

**Verbatim (Ms Smith, the Premier, answering Mr. Nenshi):**

> Well, Mr. Speaker, let's talk about being disingenuous. If his conspiracy theory is that someone gerrymandered the advisory panel's maps to benefit the UCP, then they failed miserably. Under the proposed maps if the UCP were to win the popular vote by the same margins as we did in 2023, nine points in total, the NDP would gain three seats and the UCP would gain one. He's being ridiculous.

Later in the same exchange:

> Why would we restore two rural districts? Because we don't want to deprive rural Alberta from effective representation. If you want to have a look at the weird map that was drawn by Notley, just look at Cardston-Siksika. If you turn it to the side, it kind of looks like Daffy Duck. No wonder it stuck out. The commission looked at it, and they decided to give Lethbridge four seats, and that's what we're going to be debating in this Legislature.

**Checked against the audit (2023 votes, all ballots; `findings/p338_projection/out/results.json`, `check_2023`):**
- 2023 actual on the current 87-seat map: UCP 49, NDP 38.
- The panel's 91-seat map (reconstruction): UCP 51, NDP 40, so +2 each. The Premier's figures imply 50–41 (UCP +1, NDP +3), one seat from ours. Seven seats on the reconstruction are decided by under 2 points, so a one-seat difference is within its error. Her figures match the panel members' alternative exactly (50–41).
- The comparison depends on the baseline. Against the current map, the four new seats split roughly evenly (ours) or 3–1 for the NDP (hers). Against the commission's 89-seat map (48–41), the panel's map gives the UCP +2 or +3 and the NDP 0 or −1.
- "The commission … decided to give Lethbridge four seats": the four-way Lethbridge split is the advisory panel's. The commission's report (its majority) kept Lethbridge as two city divisions; the commission's dissenting minority also proposed a four-way split.

## Government Motion 45: the Assembly concurs in the panel's map (added 2026-11-13 14:20 Alberta time · claude-fable-5-2 · high)

Read from the same PDF (`alberta_hansard_2026-11-04_afternoon.pdf`). Page numbers are Hansard's printed pages.

**The motion (p. 2097), moved by Mr. Amery, the Minister of Justice:**

> A. Be it resolved that the Legislative Assembly concur in the proposals of the independent advisory panel as set out in its report entitled Report to the Legislative Assembly of Alberta, October 2026, Review of the Electoral Boundaries of Alberta and Electoral Division Proposals, which was tabled by the chair of the Select Special Committee on Electoral Boundaries on November 2, 2026, Sessional Paper 637/2025-26, and which are based on the independent advisory panel's mandate pursuant to Government Motion 37;
> B. and be it further resolved that the Legislative Assembly urge the government to introduce a bill to establish new electoral divisions for Alberta in accordance with the proposals of the independent advisory panel.

**Time allocation.** Government Motion 46 limited further consideration of Motion 45 to one hour (pp. 2102 onward).

**The vote (p. 2116):** "Totals: For – 45 Against – 36 [Government Motion 45 carried]". The Assembly adjourned at 5:58 p.m.

**The Leader of the Opposition, Mr. Nenshi, in the debate (p. 2101):**

> Of course it has to be legal and constitutional; otherwise, we end up in court.

> This commission couldn't help itself, and now we have a map that is clearly gerrymandered, that is clearly illegal, that is clearly unconstitutional. The government has one last chance to fix this, and that last chance is to throw this map out and do what they should have done six months ago, which is adopt the majority maps.

These are a party leader's words in debate. The audit quotes them as his position. It does not adopt them: the audit calls no map a gerrymander and gives no legal verdict.

**Not in this Hansard:** a statement that the Opposition will file a court challenge. News reports say so (for example CBC, "Alberta to lawyer up if NDP challenges electoral boundary report: Smith"), but news sites could not be fetched from the cloud session, so that claim is not yet used on the site.

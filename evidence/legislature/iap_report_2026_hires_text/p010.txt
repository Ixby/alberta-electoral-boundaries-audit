The advisory panel also reviewed the Calgary electoral divisions and recommends that the Legislature
consider changes to Calgary-North West (to include adjacent Bearspaw communities), Calgary-Bow
(to include North Springbank) and Calgary West (to include that portion of South Springbank that the
Commission did not include when it extended the boundaries of Calgary West into Rocky View County
to take in what the Commission called Elbow Valley). The Calgary electoral division proposals are not
proposals that are supported by the entire panel but are supported by the majority.

The changes the advisory panel recommends for west Calgary, if approved, would precipitate changes to
Banff-Kananaskis or Canmore-Banff as the Commission proposed it, and also to Cochrane-Springbank as
the Commission mapped it. And so the panel has proposed changes to those two electoral divisions for the
Legislature's consideration.

In Calgary, the advisory panel also reviewed and took a hard look at the northeast Calgary electoral divisions
and came to the conclusion that short of substantial revisions to about a dozen electoral divisions in Calgary,
there were no improvements to recommend which would quickly achieve better effective representation.
However, the panel's analysis of these electoral divisions did suggest a review by the Legislature or by a
future electoral boundaries commission to determine whether effective representation might be better
achieved by a different configuration of electoral boundaries.

The advisory panel also reviewed the southern Alberta electoral divisions and has made proposals for
a reconfiguration of the Lethbridge, Lethbridge-Little Bow, Lethbridge-Taber-Warner and Lethbridge-
Cardston electoral divisions. The panel also proposes a reconfiguration of the current Livingstone-Macleod
electoral division, which was made possible by the proposed mapping of the Lethbridge-Little Bow and the
Lethbridge-Taber-Warner electoral divisions. Again, these proposals are not supported by all members of the
advisory panel. However, the majority proposes that the Legislature give these suggested reconfigurations
serious consideration.

Finally, as indicated above, the advisory panel examined the perennial problem of the Banff-Kananaskis or
Banff-Canmore electoral division which has challenged prior electoral boundaries commissions. The panel
has proposed an electoral division for the Legislature's consideration which attempts to address these
challenges: Banff-Canmore-Diamond Valley. It is not a proposal which has the support of the entire panel,
although it is supported by the majority.

Maps and detailed narratives explaining the advisory panel's proposals and the reasons for its proposal
come later in this report.

With respect to all of the panel's proposals, we would urge the Legislature to assess them on their merits, not
on which panelists supported or opposed them, or even how many panelists supported or opposed them.
The test is whether they better achieve fair and effective representation.

With respect to the minority opinion of two members of the advisory panel, all I can say as Chairman is
that I failed miserably to have this panel confine itself to the merits or otherwise of its electoral boundaries
proposals, whether those proposals were those of the majority, the minority or even a single member of
the panel. Instead, we had the same sanctimonious passing of judgment found in the majority's criticism
of the minority in the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission Report. Passing judgment on the
constitutionality of this panel's process and proposals is for others to do. It certainly was not a part of the
panel's mandate.

The minority's opinion can be summarized quite simply. Our proposals are constitutional; yours are not. Our
proposals are non-partisan; yours are not.

As a "lay" Chairman, I spent hours trying to understand the process of electoral boundary making. Having
gained a modicum of understanding, I spent many more hours listening to both sides argue the merits or
otherwise of their electoral boundary proposals. As a consequence, the only thing I can assure the Legislature
is that the proposals made in this report are worthy of the Legislature's consideration on their merits.

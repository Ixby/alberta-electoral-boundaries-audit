Calgary-North West-Bearspaw

Calgary-North West is an electoral division bordering Calgary's city limits in northwest Calgary. It was not
changed by the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission despite submissions requesting that the
western boundary of the electoral division be moved westward to include the adjacent Rocky View County
communities of Bearspaw Village, Watermark and Church Ranches which the submitters contended were
functionally integrated with City of Calgary communities to the east through schools, sports, churches,
markets and shopping, and with the relationships running in both directions.

The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission gave "serious consideration" to moving "Bearspaw"
into the Calgary-North West electoral division, Bearspaw being an ill-defined area northeast of Calgary
anchored by Highway 1A. The independent advisory panel infers from the foregoing that that the proposed
amalgamation merits consideration which the panel has now given it. Once again, this is not gerrymandering
by the panel; it was and is a proposal that was considered to have merit by both the 2025-2026 Electoral
Boundaries Commission and the advisory panel.

The electoral division as recommended by both the 2010 and the 2017 Electoral Boundaries Commissions
and which the 2025-2026 Electoral boundaries Commission declined to alter includes the Calgary
communities of Royal Oak and Rocky Ridge north of Crowchild Trail N.W. and Scenic Acres and Tuscany
south of Crowchild Trail S.W.

Significantly, the electoral division already crosses 12-Mile Coulee Road at its south end to include portions
of communities known as Bearspaw Meadows and Rockland Park which is very much an urban subdivision.
And the reason is that the City limits themselves cross 12-Mile Coulee Road in a "fish hook" shape to
incorporate two Bearspaw communities. And so the electoral division recommended by not only the 2025-
2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission, but also by the 2017 Electoral Boundaries Commission and the
2010 Electoral Boundaries Commission did in fact cross 12-Mile Coulee Road at the south end to take in the
two Bearspaw communities, both adjacent to the Bearspaw Reservoir on the Bow River.

The reason the boundaries of the City of Calgary cross 12-Mile Coulee Road near the Bow River, we suspect,
is to give the City control of its largest fresh water reservoir created by the Bearspaw Dam on the Bow River.

If the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission had dug a little deeper, they would have been aware of
the fact that the electoral boundaries west of Tuscany did not stop at Tuscany or the 12-Mile Coulee Road.
The electoral boundaries had already jumped 12-Mile Coulee Road at its south end to incorporate two
Bearspaw communities: Bearspaw Meadows and Rockland Park.

Although the Calgary Bow electoral division included Bearspaw Meadows and Rockland Park, it did
not include immediately adjacent communities to the north, namely Watermark and Bearspaw Village,
Bearspaw Pointe, Blueridge Mountain or Blueridge Estates. These Rocky View Country subdivisions are all
south of Crowchild Trail N.W. (the four-lane divided Highway 1A to Cochrane). These communities either
abut Calgary or are situated very close to it.

The 2025-2026 Electoral boundaries Commission's reason for declining to move Bearspaw into the electoral
division was simply that they saw no compelling reason to and therefore elected to maintain the status quo.
However, in the Commission's Final Report, there was no apparent consideration of the relative strengths
of the ties these residents might have to northwest Calgary versus Cochrane. Clearly the ties to Calgary
are greater. And conversely, clearly the ties of northwest Calgarians in northwest to Bearspaw and the
recreational areas nearby are strong. Two busy golf courses, riding arenas, Haskayne Legacy Park and the
Glenbow Ranch Provincial Park are places where thousands of Calgary residents recreate on a regular basis,
to say nothing of farmers' markets along Highway 1A where Calgarians shop for fresh produce.

The case for moving Church Ranches and several other Bearspaw neighborhoods north of Highway 1A into
a Calgary riding is, arguably, as strong as that for moving the neighborhoods south of the Highway 1A into
Calgary Bow. But it is not as clear cut. Issues arise as one moves north of Highway 1A.

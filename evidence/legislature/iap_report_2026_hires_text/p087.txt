Lethbridge-Little Bow

The electoral division of Little Bow has been in existence as a provincial constituency since 1913. It has
typically extended north from Lethbridge up Highways 23 and 24 capturing places like Nobleford, Barons,
Carmangay, Champion, Vulcan and that connect Picture Butte with Enchant, Travers Reservoir, Lomond,
Milo, Arrowwood, across the Bow River and the Siksika Reserve to Gleichen and Cluny. Significantly it also
includes the Town of Coalhurst.
This electoral division gets its name from the Little Bow River into which much of the water from the
Highwood River near High River is diverted south for irrigation purposes. Water from the Bow River is
also diverted into this area for irrigation purposes and is managed by the Bow River Irrigation District
headquartered in Vauxhall. And with the construction of the Twin Valley Dam and Reservoir, together with the
Badger and Travers Reservoirs, the electoral division is very much an irrigation district.
The 2017 Electoral Boundaries Commission replaced the Little Bow electoral division with a much larger
hour-glass shaped electoral division, Cardston-Siksika, that ran from Cardston and the Kainai (Blood) First
Nation Reserve all the way north to the Siksika First Nation Reserve east of Calgary. A consolidation of seven
existing Southern Alberta electoral divisions into six was deemed to be required by the 2016-2017 Electoral
Boundaries Commission because of low population-growth in Southern Alberta. And so Cardston and the
Blood Reserve were taken out of Cardston-Taber-Warner and put in a Little Bow electoral division called
Cardston-Siksika.
The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission, in its review of the Cardston-Siksika electoral division
acknowledged "the undesirable boundaries of Cardston-Siksika" into which Little Bow had been absorbed.
The Commission's solution was to recommend an electoral division, High River-Vulcan-Siksika that
absorbed much of Little Bow but also extended the electoral division all the way north to the Red Deer River.
The High River-Vulcan-Siksika electoral division stretched from Claresholm to the southern boundary of the
Town of Drumheller. Still not an ideal electoral division, but one with sufficient population to warrant the
creation of an electoral division.
The same rural Southern Alberta population deficiency that has existed for years persists to this day.
One way to solve the deficiency is create larger and larger electoral divisions which run the risk of mixing
populations with different communities of interest. The other is to "borrow population" from nearby urban
municipalities which share the same communities of interest as their rural (agrarian) neighbours.
Re-establishing a Little Bow electoral division would restore a historically coherent voice for communities
tied by Highways 23 and 24, irrigation, intensive agriculture and the Lethbridge-area agricultural services
including contiguous portions of eastern Lethbridge along Highway 3 and a contiguous portion of western
Lethbridge, again along Highway 3.
The proposed electoral division therefore would provide population balance and "connects" the rural
electoral division to the urban services, employment and processing capacity in Lethbridge upon which
much of the Little Bow population depends.

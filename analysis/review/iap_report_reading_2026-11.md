# Reading of the Independent Advisory Panel report (2026)

## 0. Header

- **Document read:** the 2026 Independent Advisory Panel (IAP) report on Alberta electoral boundaries, 308 PDF pages. Report to the Legislative Assembly, October 2026; transmittal letter dated 22 Oct 2026.
- **Source file:** the hi-res PDF, SHA-256 `d8ae78abcec3660f8158a327a25df7508b649ce5595788e8b265b362b2a75122`, from https://librarysearch.assembly.ab.ca/client/en_CA/search/asset/236105/1. The text layer was used. The low-res copy, SHA-256 `b65ee345…6f0a`, is in `evidence/legislature/iap_report_2026_lowres.pdf`.
- **Method:** every page was read, in three parts: (A) PDF 1-118, front matter, Executive Summary, section 3 proposals and sections 4-12 (the majority opinion); (B) PDF 119-170, section 13 Minority Opinion and the start of Appendix 1; (C) PDF 171-308, the rest of the appendices, the composite maps and the 91 district maps. Map pages have a jumbled text layer; the only map datum treated as reliable is the printed "Estimated Population" line, checked against Appendix 7. Where a claim rests on a map image rather than text, it is marked.
- **Citation convention:** p.N (PDF M), where N is the printed page and M the PDF page (printed page = PDF page - 6). Minority paragraphs are cited as ¶ with the printed page, for example ¶35, p.122 (PDF 128). Appendix pages are cited the same way.
- **Quotes:** every verbatim quote in this document was matched against the page text files (`txt/pNNN.txt`). Inferences are marked "(inference)"; arithmetic done from Appendix 7 is marked "(computed)".
- **Terms:** "the majority" is the three-member majority of the panel (Chair Brian O'Ferrall, Darwin K. Durnie, Monte Solberg), "the minority" is Gerard Kennedy and Brent Robinson (the identification of who is in the majority is from the minority's own labels in section 13 and the signatures; see section 10). "ABEBC" or "the Commission" is the 2025-2026 Alberta Electoral Boundaries Commission. "App. 7" is the Electoral Division Populations table (printed pp.205-206); average population 53,722; permitted band 40,292 to 67,152 (the minority writes 67,153).
- **Standing rule for this document:** no verdict is given on whether any map is a gerrymander. Where the report's authors make that claim or deny it, the claim is recorded as theirs.

## 1. Section-by-section summary of the report

**Front matter (PDF 1-8, printed pp.-5 to 2).** Cover. Transmittal letter of 22 Oct 2026 to Mr. Lunty and the Select Special Committee, signed by the five members; it refers to Motion 37, to the 91 electoral divisions, says the panel's proposals incorporate Recommendation 5 "subject to modifications", and states that a Minority Opinion of two members is included. Contents (PDF 5-6) lists section 13 Minority Opinion at printed p.113 and Appendices 1-8. Acknowledgements thank the 2025-26 Commission and the "much-maligned Minority Commissioners"; the Legislature added two divisions.

**Executive Summary (pp.3-5, PDF 9-11).** The panel was appointed 9 June 2026 under Government Motion 37 (approved 21 April 2026). It restored Lac Ste. Anne and Rocky Mountain House-Sundre, listed the cascading changes, and created Edmonton-Nisku and Banff-Canmore-Diamond Valley. The Calgary and southern Alberta proposals and Banff-Canmore-Diamond Valley are "supported by the majority" but not the whole panel. For north-east Calgary the panel found "no improvements to recommend" short of revising about a dozen divisions, and suggests a later review. The Chair says the proposals are "not unconstitutional", are not a collateral attack, are within the mandate, and calls the minority opinion a "40-page polemic".

**Section 3, the proposed electoral divisions (pp.6-90, PDF 12-96).** One map page and one narrative page per division, in this order:
- Lac Ste. Anne (restored, with Stony Plain; Rec. 5 restoration), pp.6-8.
- The cascade: Spruce Grove (p.10-11), Morinville-Sturgeon (pp.12-13), Barrhead-Westlock-Athabasca (pp.14-15), Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche (pp.16-17), West Yellowhead (pp.18-19), Drayton Valley-Devon (pp.20-21).
- Leduc-Beaumont (restored, unanimous; pp.22-24) and the new Edmonton-Nisku (pp.26-27), with Edmonton-South, -Ellerslie, -Meadows, -Windermere, -Whitemud and -Strathcona returned to the shapes of the Commission's Interim Report or the current map (pp.28-39).
- Rocky Mountain House-Sundre (restored under s.15(2); pp.40-42) and the Highway 2 corridor cascade: Wetaskiwin-Ponoka, Lacombe-Sylvan Lake, Blackfalds-Innisfail, Olds-Didsbury-Three Hills, Drumheller-Stettler (pp.44-53).
- A signed discussion paper, "Northeast Calgary's Boundary Dilemma" (Durnie, Solberg; pp.55-57), proposing a four-division north-east Calgary discussion position but not adopted as a map.
- Calgary west: Calgary-North West-Bearspaw, Calgary-Bow-Springbank, Calgary-West-Springbank (pp.58-68); majority-only.
- Cochrane given its own division (pp.70-71).
- "The Case for Regional Representation in Southern Alberta" (pp.73-76), then Lethbridge (one Lethbridge-only division) and three Lethbridge "hybrid" divisions (Lethbridge-Little Bow, Lethbridge-Taber-Warner, Lethbridge-Cardston) and Macleod-Crowsnest (pp.78-87); majority-only ("three of the five").
- Banff-Canmore-Diamond Valley, drawn to avoid s.15(2) status (pp.88-90); majority-only.

**Section 4, Mandate (pp.91-94, PDF 97-100).** The majority reads Motion 37 clause C(e)(i) as giving the panel the full commission function (to "review ... and make proposals"), says it did not sit in appeal of the Commission and was not bound by its proposals, treats Recommendation 5 as incorporated "subject to any modifications", and says the minority's narrower reading would make the clause superfluous. It says the minority members "are the ones who have exceeded the panel's mandate".

**Section 5, Recommendation 5 (pp.95-96, PDF 101-102).** Characterises Chair Miller's Rec. 5 as a "bargain" with the Legislature; "does not deserve deification".

**Section 6, Unnecessary and Counter-Productive Controversy (pp.97-99, PDF 103-105).** Criticises the Commission majority's reply to its minority and the panel minority's allegations. Defines gerrymandering by motive, warns against reliance on vote "transpositioning", and says of the minority "They purport to be non-partisan. They are not."

**Section 7, Population (pp.101-102, PDF 107-108).** Uses the 2024 mid-year estimate, Alberta total 4,888,723, average 53,722, band 40,292-67,152, populations from Elections Alberta (full quote in section 7 of this document).

**Section 8, Population Parity (pp.103-104, PDF 109-110).** Parity and effective representation are the only two mandatory considerations; chasing parity can produce "incongruous" divisions; Commission variance statistics; 2023 seat-share comparison.

**Section 9, Effective Representation (p.105, PDF 111).** The term "has never been authoritatively defined".

**Section 10, Hybrid Electoral Divisions (pp.107-108, PDF 113-114).** Hybrids are long-standing and judicially sanctioned; 19 hybrids since 2017 plus 8 from the Commission makes 27; the panel adds "three quasi-hybrids in Calgary and three real hybrids in southern Alberta".

**Section 11, Iterative Process (pp.109-110, PDF 115-116).** The panel is "another iterative step"; a 13-step list from Commission to Legislature.

**Section 12, The Law (pp.111-112, PDF 117-118).** The majority's twelve-point statement of the law (Charter effective representation, parity, Carter, Lalande 2026 SCC 13, ABCA references of 1991 and 1994, hybrids).

**Section 13, Minority Opinion (Kennedy and Robinson; pp.113-157, PDF 119-163; 167 numbered paragraphs, 44 minority pages).**
- Executive Summary and Parts I-III (¶1-20): agrees unanimously with the majority on Lac Ste. Anne and Rocky Mountain House-Sundre; objects to the changes south of Airdrie (Springbank/Bearspaw into three Calgary divisions, four north-east/east Calgary divisions, Lethbridge, Canmore-Banff/Diamond Valley) as outside the mandate, not "necessary", and a litigation risk; calls the process "constitutionally irregular, but constitutionally permissible".
- Part IV (¶21-38): details of the two unanimous restorations and one objection (about 1,300 Red Deer residents in Blackfalds-Innisfail).
- Part V (¶39-84): mandate. Consequences of exceeding it; Motion 37 and Rec. 5 text; the Addendum's penultimate paragraph as obiter; "necessary" read with six contextual clues; alternatively "necessary" is a high standard.
- Part VI (¶85-143): hybrid typology (Tables 1-3); Springbank and Bearspaw (Table 4); north-east and east Calgary; Lethbridge; Canmore-Banff and Diamond Valley.
- Part VII (¶144-161): partisan implications; population parity (Table 5); litigation risk.
- Part VIII (¶162-167): conclusion; norms of independent commissions; a 91-division list the Legislature could adopt (68 ABEBC Majority + 8 Interim + 12 unanimous IAP + 3 minor amendments).

**Appendices (PDF 159-308).**

- App. 2, the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act, current as of 14 May 2026 (PDF 173-184).
- App. 3, Chair Miller's Addendum with Recommendation 5 (PDF 185-188).
- App. 4, the Commission's "Response to the Minority Report" (PDF 189-193).
- App. 5, panel member biographies (PDF 195-198).
- App. 6, composite maps a)-f) (PDF 199-208).
- App. 7, Electoral Division Populations (PDF 209-212).
- App. 8, provincial map plus 91 district maps (PDF 213-306).
- Back cover: 1905 electoral divisions map, "Report to the Legislative Assembly of Alberta October, 2026" (PDF 308).
- Blank pages: PDF 2, 4, 8 (footer only), 15, 31, 49, 60, 67, 75, 83, 106, 164, 166, 172, 174, 184, 186, 190, 194, 196, 200, 202, 210, 214, 307 (some of these are footer-only; the blank-page list is from the parts' page logs and is not independently re-counted).

## 5. s.15(2) cases

Statute and Motion 37 (App. 1, clause C(e)(ii)(C); App. 2, EBCA s.15(2)): up to four divisions may have a population as much as 50% below the average if at least three of five criteria exist: (1) area over 20,000 km² (or surveyed area over 15,000 km²); (2) distance from the Legislature Building to the nearest boundary by the most direct highway route over 150 km; (3) no town over 8,000 population (Crowsnest Pass is not a town); (4) contains an Indian reserve or Métis settlement; (5) a portion of the boundary coterminous with the Province's boundary. The panel's own opinion decides the 3-of-5 test. Average 53,722: -25% = 40,292; -50% = 26,861.

On the panel's map the only divisions below -25% (computed from App. 7) are Central Peace-Notley, Lesser Slave Lake and Rocky Mountain House-Sundre. Every other division is within -25%. The report's maps print no highway distances and no town populations, so criteria (2) and (3) cannot be tested from the maps.

## 7. Sources cited by the panel

### 7.1 Population base, section 7 (pp.101-102), in full

Printed p.101 (PDF 107):

> 7. Population
>
> By law, the population of a proposed electoral division must not be more than 25% above and not more than 25% below the average population of all the proposed electoral divisions: Section 15(1) of the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act.
>
> As a consequence, the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act prescribes rules for determining of the Province's population. Boundaries commissions are to use the most recent Federal Government "decennials census". But the results of the most recent decennial census (2026) were not available to the Commission, nor were they available to the advisory panel. They will not be released until 2027. In that event, the Act permits an electoral boundaries commission to use "a province-wide census that is more recent than the decennial census"; so long as it is used in conjunction with information provided in the Federal Government's last decennial census. See Section 12 of the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act in the Appendix to this report for the hierarchy of rules governing the determination of the population of Alberta. The rules are dictated by what census information is available and how recent that information is.
>
> Electoral boundaries commissions have historically struggled deciding on appropriate population figures. The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission struggled with the issue, but aided by some very good statisticians and demographers, they relied on the 2024 mid-year population estimates which were released in May of 2025 or about the time the Commission got started. The Commission was unanimous in its decision to use these population figures. The Commission used the number of 4,888,723 for Alberta's population as the most accurate and verified population that it had at its disposal.
>
> The advisory panel too was unanimous in using that population figure for several reasons. The first was that the Federal Government's decennial census results will not be released until 2027. Even the mid-2025 provincial population estimates were not scheduled to be released until after the advisory panel was well into its mandate. The advisory panel was appointed in June 2026. The mid-2025 population estimates were not scheduled to be released until July 2026. Indeed, the advisory panel is not aware whether they have yet been released.
>
> Another reason for the panel to rely on the 2024 mid-year population estimates the Commission used was so that comparisons between what the advisory panel recommended and what the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission recommended might be made on the basis of the same population estimates.
>
> Of course, in seeking population parity or relative population parity, or "population balancing" as it is sometimes called, one needs to know not only the population of the Province in order to divide it by the number of electoral divisions for the average population of the proposed electoral division, but also the population of the proposed electoral division being considered. The advisory panel relied on Elections Alberta for the populations of the electoral divisions it proposed. Elections Alberta has software that instantly produces population numbers for proposed electoral division configurations the panel was considering. The proposed electoral boundaries can be manipulated on a computer screen, and the resultant population instantly pops up.
>
> Elections Alberta maps population for so-called "dissemination areas" which are geographic areas defined by Statistics Canada for census purposes. Statistics Canada apparently has defined roughly 6,000 dissemination areas in Alberta. The Federal Government attempts to count the people in those areas every 10 years. The Provincial Government (the Office of Statistics and Information at the Alberta Treasury Board) then estimates growth or decline in the population of dissemination areas based on a multitude of indices and actual counts. Elections Alberta then maps those dissemination area populations in order that electoral boundaries commissions and others might know how many people are likely to live within a defined geographic area.
>
> There are a number of checks and cross-checks which are employed to ensure the validity of the dissemination area population estimates. In short, voters can be assured that the populations of proposed

> electoral divisions are reasonably accurate. Those population estimates are not just relied upon for electoral boundary making, they are relied upon by the Government, municipalities, health authorities, licensing authorities, school authorities, insurance companies, and other commercial enterprises for the decisions they make. In the advisory panel's view, criticisms of the Commission's use of the population estimates it used are unjustified.
>
> Having divided the Province into 91 electoral divisions, as the advisory panel was mandated to do, the panel arrived at an average population of the proposed 91 electoral divisions, employing the following formula:
>
> Total Estimated Population = The average population of the electoral division / No. of Electoral Divisions
> 4,888,723 = 53,722 / 91
>
> As a consequence, the advisory panel based its proposals on an average electoral division population of 53,722.
>
> Section 15(1) of the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act states that the population of a proposed electoral division must not be more than 25% above nor more than 25% below. Twenty-five percent of 53,722 is 13,430. Therefore, the population of an electoral division may vary from 40,292 to 67,152. (There is provision in the Act for four special consideration electoral divisions, but the 25% rule applies unless the statutory criteria for special consideration electoral divisions are met, and then only in respect of four electoral divisions.)
>
> There have been suggestions that electoral boundaries mapping should be based on the number of electors in any given electoral division. That would require an amendment to the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act which states that it is the population, not the number of electors, which must meet the plus or minus 25% rule. That is, it is population, not electors, which an electoral boundaries commission and the advisory panel must consider in recommending electoral boundaries.
>
> In the last general election only 60% of those registered to vote voted and voter turnout percentages varied widely from one electoral division to the next. They also vary widely over time. But, regardless, once elected, an MLA is supposed to or assumed to represent everyone in the electoral division, not just those who voted. And that is a reason why population is used to determine the areas of electoral divisions. Basing electoral boundaries on numbers of actual voters, or on the number of people registered to vote, is something which would have to be studied carefully. It is not unprecedented, but the panel is not in a position to recommend it or recommend against it.

Reading notes on the passage (inference unless stated): the figure 4,888,723 is the 2024 mid-year estimate; the per-division populations come from Elections Alberta's mapping of Statistics Canada dissemination-area populations, which the Alberta Treasury Board's Office of Statistics and Information updates from the last decennial census (presumably 2021; the passage does not say). The sum of the 91 App. 7 populations is exactly 4,888,723 (computed). The passage calls the 2026 census "the most recent decennial census"; the panel did not have it.

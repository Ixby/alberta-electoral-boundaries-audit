Minority Opinion - Panel of the Special Select Committee on Electoral Boundaries  1

Executive Summary

We, Gerard Kennedy and Brent Robinson, have had the opportunity to review portions of the draft report of
our fellow panelists, the Honourable Brian O'Ferrall (Chair), Darwin Durnie, and the Honourable Monte
Solberg. We are grateful to have discussed and reviewed these matters with them in good faith. It is
encouraging that the Panel has mostly achieved unanimity in how to restore two rural electoral
divisions that the Alberta Electoral Boundaries Commission ("ABEBC") recommended removing.
Those two electoral divisions are:

1) Lac Ste. Anne: a successor electoral division to Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland; and
2) Rocky Mountain House-Sundre: a successor electoral division to Rimbey-Rocky-Mountain House-Sundre,

     which we recommend receive protection under s 15(2) of the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act, RSA 2000,
     c E-3.

The restoration of Lac Ste. Anne has cascading impacts on fourteen electoral divisions, eight of which are
recommended to have boundaries proposed in the ABEBC Interim Report. We unanimously agree with our
colleagues not only on these boundaries, but also that the restoration of Lac Ste. Anne is constitutionally
compliant. It improves effective representation for residents of all affected electoral divisions and the province
as a whole.

We are of the further opinion that the restoration of Rocky Mountain House-Sundre can be constitutionally
compliant, with cascading impacts on only six other electoral divisions. We disagree with our colleagues,
however, on the boundaries of their proposed electoral divisions of Olds-Didsbury-Three Hills and Canmore-
Banff insofar as they connect to changes south of Airdrie. We also disagree, although not as strongly, with our
colleagues' proposed boundaries of Red Deer-South and Blackfalds-Innisfail.

Most importantly, we cannot agree with our colleagues' other recommended boundary changes to
other electoral divisions south of Airdrie.

First and foremost, we are of the opinion that all additional changes that our colleagues recommend to areas
south of Airdrie fall outside this Panel's mandate. It is our view that Motion 37, which empowers this Panel,
provides a very narrow mandate: incorporate Recommendation 5 of ABEBC Chair Justice Dallas Miller.
Essentially, the Panel is to recommend how to restore the two electoral divisions that the majority of the
ABEBC recommended be removed, thereby generating a map of 91 electoral divisions for the next provincial
election. The Panel must also follow the strict non-partisan terms prescribed by Justice Miller
(including specific maps of Calgary, Edmonton, and Lethbridge) and the longstanding norms of
electoral boundaries commissions in Canada. Recommendation 5 seeks to preserve as many electoral
boundaries as is practically feasible from the ABEBC Majority Report. Specifically, Recommendation 5
stipulates that expanding the Legislature to 91 seats and adding two rural seats should "have no impact on any
electoral division in Airdrie or containing territory south of Airdrie's southernmost border, with the exception
of Drumheller-Stettler".

Any changes to the terms prescribed by Justice Miller must be "necessary": in our view, such changes must be
necessary to restore the two rural electoral divisions. Our colleagues have interpreted the mandate provided to
the Panel in Motion 37 to recommend "necessary" changes more broadly. Even if that interpretation is accurate,
however, any additional unrelated boundary change must be considered "necessary" to achieve effective
representation as guaranteed by s 3 of the Charter. This is still a very narrow mandate, which our colleagues
have interpreted unreasonably broadly.

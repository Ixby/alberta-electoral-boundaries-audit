Minority Opinion - Panel of the Special Select Committee on Electoral Boundaries  8

26. Because Stony Plain and portions of Parkland County have been removed from Stony Plain-Drayton
     Valley, Drayton Valley-Devon can be recreated, with its border extending all the way east to Highway 2.
     Highway 2 is a logical border. This electoral division is also closer to the status quo, with clear transportation
     links between its two largest municipalities.

27. As a result of these changes, Spruce Grove electoral division becomes smaller, concentrating on the city
     and rural areas immediately around it. This is eminently justifiable given the exceptional growth in the area.

28. The collapse of Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland forced Leduc into an electoral division with Devon. This no longer
     being necessary, Leduc-Beaumont can be recreated, which was the overwhelming desire of residents of
     both Leduc and Beaumont. In any event, Beaumont leaving Edmonton was a term of Recommendation
     5, which we have been tasked to incorporate. The Panel considers the high population justified given the
     extensive public desire for this and clear shared interests between Leduc and Beaumont. We are reluctant
     to condone populations nearly 20% above the provincial average, for reasons discussed below. However,
     unlike other areas where the ABEBC Minority Report submitted that populations should exceed the
     provincial average by significant amounts, the residents of Leduc and Beaumont have overwhelmingly
     asked to be paired in the same electoral division. This overwhelming desire, despite the dilution of the
     votes of residents, indicates that this pairing truly is the best way to achieve effective representation. These
     cities south of Edmonton have a distinct non-Edmonton feel, meaning that effective representation, and
     the links between the cities, warrants their being in the same electoral division.

29. To prevent the population of Leduc-Beaumont being too high, the Panel recommends the creation of an
     "Edmonton-Nisku", consisting of: a) Edmonton-South East as outlined in the Interim Report; and b)
     Nisku and surrounding rural areas north of Leduc/Airport Road, east of Edmonton International
     Airport/Highway 2, and west of Beaumont's western border. Everything else south of the North
     Saskatchewan River in Edmonton can thus revert to the map outlined in the ABEBC Interim Report. This
     is part of the terms of implementing Recommendation 5. This is a better map of Edmonton, as electoral
     divisions become smaller. The electoral divisions further follow major roadways and waterways more
     logically. In particular, it does not become necessary for any electoral division to cross Gateway Boulevard,
     which is a major divider of communities in South Edmonton. Edmonton-Whitemud can further remain
     entirely north of Anthony Henday Drive, which is also a major divider in this area of Edmonton.

30. Finally, because of the restoration of Lac Ste. Anne, West Yellowhead can lose all portions of Lac Ste.
     Anne County to become a smaller electoral division more similar to its current status quo.

31. In sum, the creation of Lac Ste. Anne electoral division is constitutional in substance as well as in process.
     It improves effective representation for Edmonton and the rural areas near it. If a court, at a future date,
     holds that this process is unconstitutional for offending s 3 of the Charter, the Panel would urge the court
     to hold that this creation of Lac Ste. Anne (and the aforementioned cascading effects) is a reasonable limit
     on s 3, justifying a map of 90 electoral divisions. Several pressing and substantial objectives are served by
     this electoral division's creation:

a. Preserving rural representation;
b. Preserving Indigenous representation;
c. Being fair to Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland, whose residents were not consulted about their electoral

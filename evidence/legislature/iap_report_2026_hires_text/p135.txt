59. Recommendation 5 must further be read in the context of Recommendations 1-4, each of which is
     formulated as a single paragraph. Indeed, it is clear that Justice Miller wished that Recommendation 5 had
     been agreed to by Mr. Clark and Ms. Samson, such that it could have appeared in the Majority Report.
     Reading the Addendum's penultimate paragraph as part of Recommendation 5 is not reading the ABEBC
     Majority Report as a whole. Clauses in statutes cannot be read in isolation: Rizzo & Rizzo Shoes Ltd
     (Re), [1998] 1 SCR 27, noting importance of "harmonious" interpretation of statutes. Recommendation
     5 has been incorporated into this Panel's mandate and principles of statutory interpretation should
     therefore apply to our interpreting it: see also Vavilov at paras 115-124.

60. Moreover, Justice Miller wrote that Drumheller-Stettler was to be the only exception to boundary
     amendments to an electoral division containing territory south of Airdrie. He would not have listed a single
     exception if he also condoned changes to Calgary and Lethbridge. The expression of one exception to a
     general rule is the exclusion of other exceptions to that general rule: see Canada v Loblaw Financial
     Holdings Inc, 2021 SCC 51 at para 59, per Justice Côté (interpreting the Income Tax Act, but the principle
     is sound in different contexts, being another principle of statutory interpretation we must consider as per
     Vavilov). The government caucus seemed to understand that--as we discuss above at paragraph 58 and
     below at paragraph 80.

61. More generally, reading the penultimate paragraph as part of Recommendation 5 would destroy Justice
     Miller's purposes of restoring the two rural seats on as non-partisan terms as possible, and preserving the
     ABEBC Majority Report to the extent possible.

62. Interpreting the paragraph on the top of page 67 as non-binding musings is also the only way to render
     the Addendum coherent. Otherwise, Justice Miller would have been imposing restrictions on the
     restoration of the two rural electoral divisions and then immediately backing away from them. It is
     presumed that legal texts are meant to be coherent (see Murphy v Welsh; Stoddard v Watson, [1993] 2
     SCR 1069 at 1079, per Major J), so the paragraph on the top of page 67 must not be part of
     Recommendation 5.

63. Our interpretation is the only way to make sense of the purpose of the Addendum as a whole--textually
     and purposively. Our conclusion is strengthened by Justice Miller's final paragraph of the Addendum,
     where he emphasizes that "this Addendum in no way detracts from the majority report representing the
     views of a majority of the Commission"--again indicating his fidelity to the ABEBC Majority Report.

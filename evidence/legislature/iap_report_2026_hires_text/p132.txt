50. The mandate to incorporate Recommendation 5 was included by the legislature to guide the review. It
     would have been redundant to include this if the review could lead to changes more generally, especially
     in the context of maintaining electoral integrity. Very recently, in Sinclair-Desgagné v Canada (Chief
     Electoral Officer), 2026 SCC 31 ("Sinclair-Desgagné") at para 43, Justice Jamal interpreted legislation
     concerning election irregularities. He underscored the importance of avoiding interpretations that would
     render the legislature's words surplusage, especially when electoral fairness is at issue. It is possible to
     conduct a review of the electoral boundaries of Alberta and, in doing so, adopt the ABEBC Majority maps
     without recommending changes south of Airdrie. However, it is impossible to incorporate
     Recommendation 5 while also recommending changes to the ABEBC Majority maps to electoral divisions
     south of Airdrie. Accordingly, our interpretation of Motion 37 is the only coherent reading of this Panel's
     mandate. As we discuss below, it is to be assumed that legal mandates are coherent. The IAP Majority's
     interpretation of the mandate--that we are to incorporate Recommendation 5 but can also recommend
     changes to electoral divisions south of Airdrie--is, respectfully, incoherent.

51. Incorporating the recommendations provided in Recommendation 5 is the operative clause of paragraph
     (e)(ii)(B). We will return to the qualifying clauses below. A thorough legal interpretation of
     Recommendation 5, which has been incorporated as the heart of the legislative motion, is going to first
     be necessary. This will demonstrate that we cannot both "incorporate Recommendation 5" in our review
     and also make recommendations to changes to electoral divisions south of Airdrie.

C. Interpreting Recommendation 5

52. Justice Miller's Recommendation 5, which this Panel has been tasked to implement, was clear that the
     restoration of the two rural electoral divisions must preserve the ABEBC Majority map of the rest of the
     province to the extent possible. Specifically, he wrote:

In the circumstance that the Legislative Assembly of Alberta finds that it cannot concur in the
proposals of the majority of the Electoral Boundaries Commission in its final report to remove two
electoral divisions from areas outside of the Cities of Edmonton and Calgary, the majority of the
Commission recommends that the Assembly make amendments to the Electoral Divisions Act, increasing
the number of electoral divisions from 89 to 91 for the next general election, and restore the two
electoral divisions that this Commission has removed. The rest of the province as we propose
must be maintained to the extent possible. This would need to be studied by an all-party Select
Special Committee or other equivalent Legislative Committee in accordance with its Standing Orders.
However, restoration of the two electoral divisions should:

a) have no impact on any electoral division in Airdrie or containing territory south of
     Airdrie's southernmost border, with the exception of Drumheller-Stettler;

b) have no impact on any electoral division north of the North Saskatchewan River in Edmonton;
c) result in all electoral divisions south of the North Saskatchewan River in Edmonton reverting to

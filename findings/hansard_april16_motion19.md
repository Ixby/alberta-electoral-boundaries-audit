# The April 16, 2026 floor debate — who criticized which report

**Purpose.** The audit's record documented the commission-era criticisms (the
chair's flags against the minority; the minority's p. 356 charges against the
majority) but had no primary source for the legislature's own debate — the
in-repo Hansard rounds predate the March 23 reports. The Motion 19 sitting
was retrieved and mined 2026-07-14.

**Extraction caveat.** The Hansard PDF is two-column and the text layer
interleaves columns; only short phrases verified contiguous in the raw text
layer are quoted, each with its page. Anyone can re-verify against the public
PDF (docs.assembly.ab.ca, legislature_31/session_2/20260416_1000_01_han.pdf).

## What the record shows

**No one criticized the majority report on the floor.**

- **The government did not attack the majority report — it claimed to be
  implementing it.** Premier Smith: "We are supporting the majority" (p. 9),
  citing the majority report's own p. 58 ("the majority believes we would
  have been able to provide Albertans with more effective representation had
  the Legislature allocated an additional two seats") and the chair's
  addendum recommendation. Government House Leader Schow: "We are adopting
  the recommendations from the report" and "we are not deleting rural
  Alberta seats" (p. 12). The government's floor position wrapped Motion 19
  in the majority chair's own words — consistent with the audit's separate
  finding that Recommendation 5 was written to dissuade adoption of the
  minority map and was geographically specific to two rural seats.
- **The opposition demanded the majority report's adoption.** "MLAs in the
  House should be voting for the one and only final report of the Electoral
  Boundaries Commission" (Nenshi, p. 7); "The only lawful and constitutional
  option for this government is to adopt the majority maps" (Irwin, p. 12);
  the motion described as "a retaliation against the independent Electoral
  Boundaries Commission for daring to do its job" (p. 6).
- **The only report criticized on the floor was the minority's — by the
  opposition.** Opposition members characterized the minority proposal as
  "cooked-up maps" that were "so shocking" and charged that the committee
  process amounts to "presenting the minority report in slow motion" (p. 9).
- **Notably, the government did not defend the minority report on the floor
  either.** Its two nominees' map went unmentioned in the government's own
  justification, which rested entirely on the chair's addendum.

## What this adds to the equal-application record

The criticisms of the majority map that the audit tested (pp. 287/356 of the
minority report) exist **only** in the minority commissioners' written pages
— no legislator, government or opposition, echoed them in the April 16
debate. The "slow motion" charge is the opposition's process accusation and
belongs to the norms lens (Casing 13's territory), not to either map's
merits. Both observations are descriptive; the audit takes no position on
any speaker's characterization.

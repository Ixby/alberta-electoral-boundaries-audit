11.Electoral Boundaries Determination is an
      Iterative Process

Those who suggest that the independent advisory panel process is unconstitutional should understand that
the determination of electoral boundaries is necessarily an iterative process. An iterative process is a process
of accomplishing something which is repetitive and which involves reviewing, proposing, testing, analyzing,
amending and refining something to improve a result. It is a process that involves a lot of reiterations.

The Electoral Boundaries Commission Act expressly requires an iterative process. The Act requires an
interim report, then a final report by a boundaries commission and then a review of the commission's
recommendations by the Legislature and ultimately either approval or approval with modification or
rejection of the commission's recommendations by the Legislature.

The commission's process itself is also iterative. An electoral boundaries commission's mandate is to review
the current electoral boundaries and then make proposals to the Legislature as to the area, boundaries and
names of the electoral divisions. But before a commission makes any proposals, it must hold public hearings to
get input from the public. Having received and considered that input, the commission then prepares an interim
report for the Legislature and the public to consider; a trial balloon so to speak. It is typically a unanimous
document, but nothing turns on that unanimity because after it is tabled, the interim report is made public and
further hearings are held to provide the opportunity for input on the area and boundaries of proposed electoral
divisions. The commission then considers that further public input and puts its proposals, unanimous or
otherwise, in a final report which is tabled in the Legislature. The commissioners proposals are then reviewed
by the Legislature and are either approved or approved with modifications or rejected.

The process, of course, cannot go on forever, but there is nothing wrong with the Legislature taking a "second
look" at what was proposed so long as that second look is done with a view to achieving a more effective
representation, not with a view to giving an electoral advantage to any political party or to any incumbent or
individual aspiring to remain or become a Member of the Legislative Assembly.

The independent advisory panel's review is merely another iterative step in the process of the Legislature
making a decision on what the electoral map of Alberta will look like at the next general election. The
advisory panel's process has been authorized by a Resolution of the Legislature in the same manner that
the Legislature authorizes an electoral boundaries commission in accordance with the provisions of the
Electoral Boundaries Commission Act.

If the Legislative Assembly approves the proposals of a commission or approves the proposals with
alterations, it must do so by resolution. The Government then must introduce a Bill to establish new electoral
divisions for the Province in accordance with the Assembly's resolution. That Bill, of course, must then go
through the normal legislative process of consideration and debate in the Legislature. Amendments may
be proposed and voted on. Further reconsideration may be required. The only requirement is that the Bill
must state that any enactment of the Bill must come into force on the day that the writ is issued for the next
general election.

In the instant case, the Legislative Assembly was presented with a Final Report which included a Majority
Report, a Minority Report and an Addendum to the Majority Report authored by the Chairman. The Act
contemplates that the commission's final report may not be unanimous. In that event, the report of the
majority is the report of the commission. Nevertheless, in the event of a strong dissent, the Legislature has
decisions to make which might reasonably call for further inquiry.

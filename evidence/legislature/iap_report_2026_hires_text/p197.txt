Biographies of the Independent Advisory Panel Members

The Honourable Brian O'Ferrall, K.C., is a retired Justice of the Alberta Court of Appeal and a former Judge
of the Provincial Court of Alberta (Small Claims Division).
Brian was raised in Edmonton (Parkallen), but worked in Calgary most of his career.
Brian holds a BA in Political Science from the University of Alberta in Edmonton, a Journalism Degree from
Carleton University in Ottawa and an LLB from the University of Alberta.
As an energy regulatory lawyer for 30 years with Ballem McDill, Bennett Jones and McLennan Ross, Brian
represented proponents and opponents of energy projects before regulatory tribunals and the courts. As a
surface rights lawyer, Brian represented landowners and expropriating authorities before the Surface Rights
Board, the Land Compensation Board and at all levels of court. As an environmental lawyer, Brian appeared
before the Natural Resources Conservation Board and the Environmental Appeal Board and was a member
of the 1990 Environmental Legislation Review Panel chaired by Banff-Cochrane MLA Brian Evans, K.C., which
led to the enactment of the Environmental Protection and Enhancement Act.
As a journalist, Brian worked for the Ottawa Citizen, the Toronto Globe and Mail, CFCN Television, CHQT,
CHQR, CHED and CKXL. As a radio and television newsman, Brian covered the last years of the Social Credit
tenure in government and the first years of Premier Lougheed's tenure. He also covered City Hall in Calgary
during portions of the mayoralities of Jack Leslie and Rod Sykes.

Darwin K. Durnie brings more than 40 years of public- and private-sector leadership in municipal
infrastructure, regional service delivery, transportation, emergency management and critical infrastructure.
His career has included extensive work with all orders of government, and community and business leaders,
giving him a practical understanding of how communities connect, collaborate and plan for growth.
Darwin was named one of the American Public Works Association's Top Ten Public Works Leaders of the Year
in 2015. He is a past president of the Canadian Public Works Association and a Public Works Leadership
Fellow. His public and community service have been recognized with the Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee
Medal and Platinum Jubilee Medal.

Gerard Kennedy is an Associate Professor and Associate Dean of Graduate Studies in the Faculty of Law
at the University of Alberta. His expertise lies at the intersection of procedural law and public law, and he
has over 40 publications in these areas. He holds a JD from Queen's University, an LLM from Harvard Law
School and a PhD from Osgoode Hall Law School. Prior to joining the U of A, he was a faculty member at the
University of Manitoba and a litigation associate at Osler, Hoskin & Harcourt LLP in Toronto. He remains an
active member of the legal profession as a member of the Alberta, Manitoba and Ontario Bars. He serves
on the Alberta Judicial Council and Federal Courts Rules Committee and chairs the Advisory Board of the
Centre for Constitutional Studies.
Professor Kennedy's expertise in constitutional and administrative law has led to his work for Ontario's Far
North Electoral Boundaries Commission in 2017 and Alberta's Electoral Boundaries Commission in 2025-
2026. He was a writer and advisor for both of these commissions, becoming familiar with Canadian case law
on redistricting.

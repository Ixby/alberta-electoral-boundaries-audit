Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche

As a consequence of the recommended restoration of the Lac Ste. Anne electoral division and the
consequent restoration of Barrhead-Westlock-Athabasca, Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche is another electoral
division which the advisory panel recommends be restored to its current boundaries.
The Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche electoral division, as mapped by the 2016-2017 Electoral Boundaries
Commission, currently runs north from the Kikino Metis Settlement south of Lac La Biche to Lac La Biche,
and then up Highway 881 paralleling the now defunct Alberta and Great Waterways Railway. The electoral
division also parallels the main highway to Fort McMurray (Highway 63). It includes places like Plamondon,
Wandering River, Janvier, Anzac, Gregoire Lake and portions of Fort McMurray, principally south of the
Athabasca River. However, it also includes two communities in the City of Fort McMurray north of the
Athabasca River south of Thickwood Boulevard, namely Martin Ridge Estates and Wood Buffalo Estates.
The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission, because of the elimination of Lac Ste. Anne and changes
made to Barrhead-Westlock-Athabasca, had to move all of Smoky Lake County and the Saddle Lake Cree
Nation Reserve into Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche. But in order to prevent this redistribution resulting in
excessive population in Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche, the Commission also recommended moving the two
City of Fort McMurray communities north of the Athabasca River (Martin Ridge Estates and Wood Buffalo
Estates) into the Fort McMurray-Wood Buffalo electoral divisions.
As a consequence of the restoration of Lac Ste. Anne and the restoration of Barrhead-Westlock-Athabasca,
moving Smoky Lake County and the Saddle Lake Cree nation into Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche is no longer
necessary. It is also no longer necessary to move the Fort McMurray communities of Martin Ridge Estates
and Wood Buffalo Estates north of the Athabasca River and south of Thickwood Boulevard into Fort
McMurray-Wood Buffalo. The Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche electoral division needs the population these
communities provide.
Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche is a very large electoral division. Unlike its neighbour to the north, Fort
McMurray-Wood Buffalo, Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche has had population deficiency issues in the past. Fort
McMurray-Wood Buffalo has not had a population issue because most of the City of Fort McMurray is in it.
Roughly speaking, Fort McMurray-Lac La Biche's western boundary is the Athabasca River. Its northern
boundary is the Athabasca and Clearwater Rivers. Its eastern boundary is the Saskatchewan border and the
Cold Lake Air Weapons Range, and its southern boundary is south of Lac La Biche. The 2025-2026 Electoral
Boundaries Commission deemed it necessary to push the southern boundary south to Smoky Lake County
and then to shed population, recommended moving its two urban communities north of the Athabasca into
Fort McMurray-Wood Buffalo.
Suffice it to say that the advisory panel recommends moving Smoky Lake County into Barrhead-Westlock-
Athabasca to give it necessary population, and, therefore, is not recommending moving Martin Ridge Estates
and Wood Buffalo Estates into Fort McMurray-Wood Buffalo.

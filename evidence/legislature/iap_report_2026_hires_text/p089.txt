Lethbridge-Taber-Warner

This is an electoral division which is mapped and organized around the Highway 3 and Highway 4 corridors
east and south of Lethbridge. Highway 3 east to Coaldale, Taber, with a detour north to Vauxhall, and on
to Bow Island. Highway 4 to the south to Warner, Milk River and the U.S. Border at Coutts is a part of the
CANAMEX Corridor, a 3,700-mile/6,000 km network of highways and transportation infrastructure linking
Alberta, the United States and Mexico. And east of Highway 4 at Stirling the electoral division extends just
south of Lethbridge along Highway 61 to Wrentham, Foremost and Manyberries.
Including a small population from Lethbridge (6,679 people) in the electoral division provides a practical
way to give this electoral division a population which meets statutory requirements and yet remains
geographically manageable. It permits a southern Alberta electoral division which does not have to become
so large that effective representation cannot be achieved.
This recommendation is also very much based on the community of interests which the Taber-Warner
folks share with the City of Lethbridge. It is an electoral division organized around the highway corridors,
irrigation and dryland farming and its produce, food processing, cross-border and shipping logistics and
shared institutions most of which are located in Lethbridge. Contiguous portions of east and southeast
Lethbridge would anchor the electoral division and reflect, among other relationships, the existing Premier
Food Corridor relationship among Lethbridge, Coaldale and Taber. The electoral division possesses a more
understandable community of shared interests than a geographically huge corridor assembled from far-
flung municipalities with limited daily interaction.
See also what two members of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission said about what they
called a Lethbridge-Taber-Warner electoral division at page 61 of their Minority Report. Their analysis was
persuasive. What the minority members of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission recommended
was not substantively unreasonable. Their recommendations did not violate the values underlying Section
3 of the Charter. Nor did they violate principles of procedural fairness. They were the subject of public input
and Commission member's questions at a number of the Commission's hearings. There may be reasons
for rejecting the recommended electoral boundaries; but those reasons should relate to their merits, their
advantages and disadvantages, not to arguably inapplicable Charter or other legal principles.

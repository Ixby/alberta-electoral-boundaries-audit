The Legislature responded to the Chairman's recommendation by urging the Government to introduce a
Bill increasing the size of the Legislature. The Legislature also appointed an independent advisory panel
to conduct a review of the Province's electoral divisions. In conducting that review, the advisory panel
was mandated to incorporate the Chairman's recommendation subject to any modifications the panel
considered necessary.

The panel has now incorporated the Chairman's recommendations, with respect to the Rocky Mountain
House electoral division. The panel is recommending that it be mapped as described below. Because of
that restoration, the panel has also been able to recommend arguably more logical electoral divisions along
Highway 2 between Calgary and Edmonton. Those recommendations come later in this report.

With respect to the current Rimbey-Rocky Mountain-Sundre electoral division, the advisory panel
recommends replacing it with a similar electoral division named Rocky Mountain House-Sundre. Rimbey is
not recommended for inclusion in the restored electoral division.

The new Rocky Mountain House-Sundre electoral division's southern boundary meanders west along a
watershed northwest of Cochrane to the B.C. Border. Its western boundary follows the B.C. Border north
to the Brazeau River and then northeast along the Brazeau River to the northern boundary of Clearwater
County which is recommended to form the northern boundary of the electoral division as well. The electoral
division includes the Saulteaux/Cree First Nations' O'Chiese and Sunchild Reserves in the north. Its eastern
boundary is roughly a north-south line just east of the Highway 22 corridor which runs north-south up the
spine of the Province's "West Country". The electoral division includes Sundre, Caroline, Rocky Mountain
House and Nordegg, but not Rimbey, which we have recommended be put in Wetaskiwin-Ponoka.

The recommended electoral division is roughly 25,000 square kilometers in size and is sparsely settled by
a population of approximately 30,000 which is about 44% below the average provincial electoral division
population. The changes we recommend would take 17,000 residents out of the current Rimbey-Rocky
Mountain House-Sundre electoral division. We are therefore recommending the new electoral division of
Rocky Mountain House-Sundre be one of the four electoral divisions permitted to have a population more
than 25% below the average population of all the proposed electoral divisions in the province. (53,722). This
also accords with a suggestion made by the 2025-2026 Commission Chairman.

The proposed electoral division meets the criteria required by Section 15 (2) of the Electoral Boundaries
Commission Act for special treatment. The area of the electoral division exceeds 20,000 square kilometres.
There is no town in the proposed electoral division with a population exceeding 8,000. The proposed
electoral division contains several First Nations' reserves. And its western boundary is coterminous with
a provincial border (namely, the B.C. border). Only three of the Section 15 (2) criteria are required. The
proposed electoral division possesses at least three of the five required criteria.

The area that we are recommending for inclusion in a restored Rocky Mountain House-Sundre electoral
division has had a Rocky Mountain House electoral division of similar size, configuration and name since at
least 1940 and so the status quo has been preserved. Preserving the status quo is a good thing in electoral
boundary mapping, absent contrary considerations of which there are none.

There are also clearly defined communities of interest across the entire electoral division. First Nations'
interests, environmental protection interests, resource extraction interests, agriculture, forestry, and
recreational tourism interests. The David Thompson Highway (Highway 11) runs through the proposed Rocky
Mountain-Sundre electoral division from Red Deer to the Banff-Jasper Highway at the Saskatchewan River
Crossing. The David Thompson, far from dividing the electoral division, unites it.

The electoral division is geographically huge and many of its roads are forestry trunk roads and so
representing some constituents is not easy. Nor is it always easy for constituents to participate in the
political process and advocate for their interests. It truly deserves special treatment.

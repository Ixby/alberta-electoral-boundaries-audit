B. The Prime Importance of Population in Drawing Electoral Divisions

147. We also note that our colleagues' changes to Calgary and Lethbridge increase population disparities. In
     small ways, to be sure. But this warrants a short discussion on the predominant place of population in
     drawing electoral divisions, beyond our colleagues' changes to Calgary and Lethbridge.

148. In the ABEBC Majority Report, Calgary districts had an average population of 56,379 or 2.64% above the
     provincial average. Edmonton districts had an average population of 58,041 or 5.67% above the provincial
     average. The other districts in Alberta had an average population of 52,281 or 4.82% below the provincial
     average.

149. If the IAP Majority districts are adopted, Calgary districts have an average population of 56,726 or 5.59%
     above the provincial average. Edmonton districts have an average population of 56,948 or 6.00% above
     the provincial average. All other districts in Alberta have an average population of 50,107 or 6.73% below
     the provincial average.

150. These disparities are exacerbated once one expands the consideration from Alberta's two largest cities to
     the four largest cities to include Red Deer and Lethbridge. If those cities are included in the "urban"
     definition, the average population of urban districts goes to 56,847 or 5.82% above the provincial average.
     There is also one fewer seat within those urban districts as they go from a total of 53 to 52 seats as the
     IAP Majority has removed an urban electoral division from Lethbridge.

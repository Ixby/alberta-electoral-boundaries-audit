122. Our colleagues object to the non-hybridization of Lethbridge on the grounds that it is disparate treatment
     from Medicine Hat. However, the most similar community in terms of population size to Lethbridge is
     not Medicine Hat--it is Red Deer. Like Lethbridge has been treated since 1971, Red Deer is placed into
     two electoral divisions that mostly follow the municipal boundaries of the community. If our panel
     members were looking for the example of ensuring that like communities of similar size and nature were
     treated the same, Red Deer is the most obvious example. In other words, we do not propose treating
     Lethbridge exceptionally, as our colleagues claim. We propose treating Lethbridge precisely the way one
     would expect a city of its size to be treated--and exactly how the other city of its size is treated. It is also
     how we expect Medicine Hat would be treated if Medicine Hat had a population of 110,000. We have also
     treated Medicine Hat and Lethbridge in another similar way that our colleagues generally support: we have
     mostly supported the status quo in both cities. There is no disparate treatment between these cities because
     they are in fundamentally different situations.

123. There were also pragmatic reasons to treat Medicine Hat and Lethbridge differently. It is mathematically
     possible, albeit pushing the envelope of legality, to have a purely urban Medicine Hat electoral division.
     This was, in fact, seemingly the strong preference of Hatters, despite the high population that would result.
     But the ABEBC, in its unanimous Interim Report, explicitly linked the decision to not have a purely urban
     Medicine Hat seat to the decision to keep Lethbridge urban. The ABEBC itself characterized this as a
     "compromise" even though the bulk of the submissions would have supported purely urban electoral
     divisions in both cities.8

124. Finally, we again note that hybrid electoral divisions were described in the 1994 Reference as one of three
     methods that could be used to deal with the ongoing differential growth rates between more urban
     communities and more rural communities. The others were to expand the size of rural electoral divisions
     or to increase the number of electoral divisions. The direction to this panel to draw 91 electoral divisions,
     up from the current 87, is already using one of those methods. We have engaged in this exercise and
     provided our advice because we believe that going to 91 electoral divisions by adding two rural electoral
     divisions represents a fair balancing of the interests of those who live in rural and urban communities. The
     recommendation by our colleagues to do something unprecedented and unnatural with hybrid electoral
     divisions upsets that balance: they have added a third rural electoral division within the map of 91. This
     favours the interests of rural residents over the expressed interests of urban residents in a way that we
     view as unfair and undermines Lethbridge residents' right to effective representation.

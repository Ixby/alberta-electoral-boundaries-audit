The minority's interpretation of the advisory panel's mandate fails to give effect to the clear and
unambiguous meaning of the words of Government Motion 37. Indeed, the minority devotes more ink
to the issue of the consequences of exceeding the mandate, the "centrality" of Recommendation 5 and
"interpreting" Recommendation 5 than they do to Government Motion 37. And the reason the minority "spills
more ink" on Recommendation 5 than on government Motion 37 is that, in their view, the advisory panel's
mandate is not defined by Government Motion 37, but rather is defined and limited by the Commission
Chairman's Recommendation 5.

The minority's argument that clause C.(e)(ii)(B) prevents the panel from reviewing all the electoral
boundaries of Alberta and making proposals with respect to their area, boundaries and names, which
Clause C.(e)(i) clearly mandated, is driven by the minority's agenda, namely to defend the electoral division
proposals of the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission at all costs. The minority admits as much:
"We essentially view this "panel's work as a continuation and completion of the ABEBC's work." The minority
argues that the advisory panel was tasked only to implement the specific directions of the judge who chaired
the Commission, despite clear language in Government Motion 37 to the contrary.

In any event, the advisory panel implemented the Chairman's Recommendation 5. The panel recommended
restoration of the two deleted electoral divisions. That was the recommendation. The other suggestions were
simply the Chairman's attempt to tie the restoration to terms which would prevent any body, including the
Legislature, from reviewing the Commission's electoral boundaries recommendations.

If the Chairman's "conditions" were binding, as the minority opinion suggests they were, then the 2025-2026
Electoral Boundaries Commission exceeded its mandate. We do not believe that even the Commission
Chairman would consider his conditions as binding on the Legislature. And they were also not binding on
the panel. The proof of that is to be found in Government Motion 37 and the panel's mandate to make any
modifications to the Chairman's Recommendation 5 it considered necessary.

The minority assumes throughout their opinion that electoral boundaries commission's proposals have
the force of law. They refuse to recognize that electoral boundaries commission proposals are just that:
proposals. They also fail to recognize that the Legislature has the ultimate responsibility for electoral
boundaries. Electoral boundaries commissions do not make "decisions" to which principles of collateral
attack apply. Nor was the advisory panel's process one of "relitigating issues" which had already been
decided. Nothing has been decided.

The minority argues that the Legislature's direction to incorporate the Chairman's recommendations subject
only to modifications the panel considered "necessary" further limited the panel's mandate. But even if the
Chairman's restoration recommendation was made subject to preserving the Commission's mapping, the
Government Motion's earlier direction to review and make proposals with respect to the Province's electoral
divisions made it "necessary" to adjust that so-called condition.

There would have been no need for an advisory panel to review and make proposals as to the areas,
boundaries and names of the Province's electoral divisions if all it was to do was incorporate the
recommendations found within Recommendation 5.

The word "incorporate" in Clause C.(e)(ii)(B) must also be given meaning. The minority opinion fails to deal
with that. The only meaning the word "incorporate" could possibly have, on the wording of Government
Motion 37, is to incorporate the Chairman's recommendations in the proposals the panel was clearly
mandated to make in Clause C.(e)(i), the first Clause in the Motion prescribing the panel's mandate. To
interpret Clause C.(e)(ii)(B), a later clause, as eviscerating the effect of a prior, more general, direction is
questionable statutory interpretation.

The panel's proposals are based on recognized principles of electoral boundary making. Those engaged in
the process of making electoral boundary proposals may weigh the relevant considerations differently. Take
a look at the "relevant considerations" in Section 14 of the Electoral Boundaries Commission Act. Those
considerations are not objective. They are subjective and weight must be attached to them. Nor do the
relevant considerations always point to the same electoral boundaries configuration.
Reasonable people can reasonably disagree. There are no right answers. There are wrong answers,
admittedly, but no right ones. It is no easy task. An imperfect solution to an impossible task is how the
2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission Chairman characterized it. We would concur.
It simply cannot be fairly said that the advisory panel's process or its proposals somehow breach recognized
principles of electoral boundary-making or the Constitution. Those allegations, assertions and opinions are
repeated throughout the minority's opinion as if they were established facts, yet no support is provided. They
have not been established. In this day and age, if one repeats an unsupported allegation or opinion often and
eloquently enough, people will start to accept the allegation or opinion as fact, particularly if others simply
unthinkingly repeat it. It is unnecessary, wrong and brings a very important process into disrepute.

Spruce Grove

This is another electoral division impacted by the changes the advisory panel is recommending in
connection with the restoration of the Lac Ste. Anne electoral division.
The current (2017) electoral division of Spruce Grove-Stony Plain contains both Spruce Grove and Stony
Plain. If the advisory panel's recommendations are adopted, the electoral division will be comprised only of
Spruce Grove and certain adjacent lands, not including Stony Plain.
The 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission recommended removing Stony Plain from Spruce Grove.
The Commission recommended putting Stony Plain into an electoral division with Drayton Valley and
proposed to call the electoral division Stony Plain-Drayton Valley.
The Commission acknowledged that the connection between Stony Plain and Drayton Valley was not
obvious; and clearly it is not. But connecting the two appears to have been driven by the elimination of the
Lac Ste. Anne-Parkland electoral division.
What the advisory panel is now proposing is to put Stony Plain into the Lac Ste. Anne electoral division and
restoring the current Drayton Valley-Devon electoral division in roughly its present configuration.
The impact of all of this is that the Spruce Grove electoral division will be comprised mostly of Spruce Grove.
It will not include Stony Plain.
Spruce Grove and the areas immediately around it warrant an electoral division of their own. Spruce Grove is
a city with a population of roughly 45,000 and is expected to grow. In the meantime, we have recommended
extending the electoral division both north and south of Spruce Grove to give it the population it needs. In
other words, to give Spruce Grove the population it needs for separate representation, we are including areas
outside of Spruce Grove. Here is an example of where an area adjacent to an urban municipality assists it
in getting more effective representation. In the south of the Province, the advisory panel recommends the
converse, namely that an urban municipality (Lethbridge) share some of its population in order that adjacent
rural populations obtain more effective representation.

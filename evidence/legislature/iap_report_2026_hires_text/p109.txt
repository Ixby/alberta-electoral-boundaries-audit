8.Population Parity

There are only two considerations that must be taken into account (i.e. are compulsory) in determining
electoral division boundaries. One is relative population parity as defined in Section 15 (1) of the Electoral
Boundaries Commission Act (the 25% plus or minus variation restriction). The other factor or consideration
which must be taken into account is the requirement for what has been called "effective representation".
Effective representation, the courts say, is what is guaranteed by the Charter. Effective representation is also
required by Alberta's Electoral Boundaries Commission Act (S.14)

Relative population parity is an objective, empirical consideration. Indeed, it is an important component
of effective representation. Effective representation, apart from population parity, is not susceptible to
empirical measurement. Effective representation can be interpreted in many different ways by those who
determine electoral boundaries, and that alone is a reason for insisting on relative voter parity as a governor
on the electoral boundary proposals.

But in fixing the boundaries of proposed electoral division, effective representation is required by law
to be considered separate and apart from population parity which it is said can sometimes hinder
effective representation.

Other relevant considerations which only may be considered in determining the areas to be included in a
fixing of the boundaries of proposed electoral divisions are set out in Section 14 of the Electoral Boundaries
Commission Act. They include:

   (a) sparsity, density and rate of growth of the population
   (b) communities of interest, including municipalities, regional and rural communities, Indian reserves and
   Metis settlements
   (c) geographical factors
   (d) the availability and means of communication and transportation between various parts of Alberta
   (e) the desirability of understandable and clear boundaries, and
   (f) any other factors the Commission considers appropriate

An additional factor which the Supreme Court identified as requiring consideration was "minority
representation". And, the Supreme Court adds, "the list [of relevant considerations] is not closed".

But why must or may these factors need to be taken into consideration?

The Supreme Court's answer is "to ensure that our legislative assemblies effectively represent the diversity
of our social mosaic."

And all of these factors, the Supreme Court says, "may justify departure from absolute voter parity in the
pursuit of more effective representation".

Suffice it to say the advisory panel has considered all of these statutory considerations and relative
population-parity in making its proposals.

Often, we were confronted with a population variance that, while within the plus or minus 25% limit, was
considerably more than what the courts have indicated they would like to see. But chasing the population
needle across the sky can also produce incongruous electoral divisions.

The Commission had variances as little as 0.2% to as high as 14% (ignoring for the moment that limited
number of special consideration electoral divisions which are permitted to have a population as much as
50% below the average population).

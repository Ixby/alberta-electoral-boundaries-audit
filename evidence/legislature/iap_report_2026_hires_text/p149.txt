116. We wish to state at the outset that our colleagues genuinely believe that they have improved effective
     representation for this area, including by preserving an urban seat for Lethbridge. With respect, however,
     they have created far more problems than they have solved by any objective, non-partisan measure.

1) Lethbridge is a Poor Candidate for Any Significant Hybridization Based on Other Hybrids in the Province

117. Lethbridge is a city of approximately 110,000 people. Its residents and leaders clearly want two MLAs,
     who are drawn together with Lethbridge concerns regarding schools, busing, infrastructure, roads, etc--all
     of which are delivered in markedly different manners from the County of 40 Mile, the Siksika Reserve, or
     even Taber or Cardston. Unlike Medicine Hat, the population math is near-perfect for Lethbridge to get
     its desire. By our estimates, residents of Lethbridge sought to preserve the city's boundaries to the extent
     possible by an over 5:1 margin in their submissions to the ABEBC. We need very good reasons to be
     discarding that view, which would disrupt a status quo that has existed for 55 years.

118. Hybridizing Lethbridge to such a significant extent does not fit into any of the three examples of hybrid
     electoral divisions that we cite above. To use examples such as Camrose, Chestermere, Cochrane, Cold
     Lake, Fort Saskatchewan, Leduc, Lloydminster, Okotoks, Spruce Grove, and Sylvan Lake to justify
     hybridizing Lethbridge is disingenuous. Doing so misunderstands and misdescribes those electoral
     divisions, which were compelled by population.

119. Similarly, unlike Airdrie, Fort McMurray, Grande Prairie, Sherwood Park, or St. Albert, Lethbridge can be
     divided into two reasonably populated electoral divisions that basically follow the city boundaries. (We
     return to Medicine Hat below.)

120. Finally, the enormous hybrids that our colleagues have proposed for Lethbridge, which extend for
     hundreds of kilometres, are clearly distinguishable from the hybrids concerning Elbow Valley, Conrich,
     Prince of Peace, Tsuut'ina, Enoch, and Nisku. The longest distance from the city border and the extreme
     end of one of these electoral divisions is 30 km. The Calgary and Edmonton hybrids are adding rural areas
     to cities to share the burden of population and reflect that the "rural" areas are actually quasi-urban. By
     contrast, there are 80 km between the border of Lethbridge and the southern border of Lethbridge-
     Cardston, 120 km to the northern border of Lethbridge-Little Bow, and 150 km to the southeastern border
     of Lethbridge-Taber-Warner. Further, the rural populations added to the urban electoral divisions in the
     large cities are relatively small compared to their overall populations. In our colleagues' proposed
     Lethbridge hybrid electoral divisions, the populations of Lethbridge being incorporated into Lethbridge-
     Cardston, Lethbridge-Taber-Warner, and Lethbridge-Little Bow actually represent the minority of the
     population of the electoral divisions. Essentially, our colleagues have taken one of Lethbridge's two urban
     seats away and replaced it with a rural electoral division of Lethbridge-Little Bow. As we note above in
     our discussion of Blackfalds-Innisfail and Red Deer, we find it unlikely that these MLAs will become
     versed in Lethbridge's issues in a way that they would be if Lethbridge were the vast majority of their
     electoral division.

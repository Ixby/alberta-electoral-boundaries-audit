B. The Centrality of Recommendation 5

45. The mandate for this Panel comes from Motion 37. In operative part, Motion 37 reads:

     (e) the independent advisory panel's mandate is to

           (i) conduct a review of the electoral boundaries of Alberta and make proposals as to the area,
           boundaries and names of the electoral divisions of Alberta.

           (ii) in conducting its review,

                 (A) divide the electoral boundaries of Alberta into 91 proposed electoral divisions

                 (B) incorporate the recommendations provided in Recommendation 5 in the Addendum to
                 the Majority Report subject to any modifications the independent advisory panel considers
                 necessary, taking into consideration the requirement for effective representation as
                 guaranteed by the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms [...] [emphases added]

46. Our colleagues have suggested that paragraphs (e)(i) and (e)(ii)(A) permit revisions to any electoral division.
     The IAP Majority suggests that, in conducting a "review" of all electoral boundaries, they may find areas
     for improvement, thereby permitting recommending changes to any electoral division. The IAP Majority
     further suggests that the "independent" status of this advisory panel may mean that the Panel must
     conduct "ab initio" review.

47. Had this been the legislature's intention, paragraph (e)(ii)(B) would have been superfluous. The legislature
     does not speak in vain: see, e.g., McDiarmid Lumber Ltd v God's Lake First Nation, 2006 SCC 58, at
     para 36. Accordingly, we must analyze Paragraph (e)(ii)(B), which clearly qualifies the manner in which
     the Panel is to conduct a review of the electoral boundaries of Alberta and divide the electoral boundaries
     into 91 proposed electoral divisions. The preface to paragraph (e)(ii) makes this clear. As we will explain,
     the mandate to incorporate Recommendation 5 heavily constrains the manner in which our review of all
     electoral divisions can be accomplished.

48. Paragraph (e)(ii)(B) instructs the Panel that the Panel is to "incorporate the recommendations provided in
     Recommendation 5 in the Addendum to the Majority Report". The IAP Majority is correct to observe
     that Recommendation 5 had no legal effect on its own. It is still not binding on the legislature. But it has
     now become binding upon this Panel as a result of Motion 37. Though this Panel is technically a body of
     the legislature, it is functionally exercising delegated authority and must therefore act within the scope of
     the delegation. The administrative law rule about ensuring that delegated power is consistent with the grant
     of authority applies across the law, including corporate law, agency law, etc. It is equally illegitimate for this
     Panel to be acting beyond the scope of its delegated authority.

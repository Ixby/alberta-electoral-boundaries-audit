6.Unnecessary and Counter-Productive Controversy

Too much of the advisory panel's time and energy were taken up with responding to the indictments of the
panel's minority who suggest that the process of the advisory panel was constitutionally reckless, an abuse
of process, and that the panel's proposals infringed the Charter. Suggestions of "inappropriate partisan
gamesmanship" and warnings about the "threat of litigation" dogged the panel throughout. The minority
appointed themselves legal counsel to the panel, though no one invited them to assume that role.

Indictments, of course, are mere allegations of wrongdoing not yet proven. And as Chairman, I saw no support
for these allegations. They were simply a continuation of the extreme, unjustified and unnecessary criticisms
of the Minority Report in the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission's Majority Report.

It is one thing to take issue with the merits of a particular electoral boundary proposal. It is quite another
to parade the horrors of a Charter breach or, worse, insinuate gerrymandering on the part of fellow
commissioners. But here is what the Commission's majority said of the minority;

       "What might be the minority's true motivation for this? Our friends south of the border have a term
       for this type of redistricting."

The classic ad hominem argument: impugn the motives of those you disagree with. But worse, it discourages
participation in the electoral boundary-making process.

That said, motives are not irrelevant in assessing electoral boundary mapping. Indeed, motivation is the
essence of gerrymandering. It is not a gerrymander to recommend or approve an electoral boundary change
that favours or disadvantages a party or a candidate. All changes to electoral boundaries have the potential
to do that. But if the change is proposed or approved for the purpose of favouring one party or candidate, then
that is prohibited gerrymandering.

Transpositioning of votes from the last election following a proposed electoral boundary change which
suggests a different electoral outcome is not proof of a gerrymander. Beware of those who base their
gerrymandering allegations on transpositioning of votes. The methodology has its weaknesses. Secrecy of the
vote prevents a transposition exercise to be 100% accurate, says Elections Canada. But more importantly, it
does not take into account even current, let alone future changes in voting preferences.

Suggestions of gerrymandering or Charter breaches are allegations which should not be made absent reliable
proof. Some evidence of motive may be found in the mapping itself; but much more is needed before such
allegations can be justified. They bring the process of electoral boundary making into disrepute.

Those who criticize an electoral boundary determination should understand that there is not an electoral map
in existence which cannot validly be criticized. But the reasons for such criticisms must be articulated in terms
of recognized electoral boundary making principles and alternatives proposed. In some jurisdictions, there
is actually a legal onus on those attacking an electoral boundary mapping scheme to present an alternate
districting scheme which will reduce or eliminate the alleged unfairness or malapportionment. The reason for
requiring alternatives is that only by comparison can unfairness be established. Mere allegations of Charter
infringement or gerrymandering against well-meaning electoral boundaries commissioners, or even against
those who make submissions to electoral boundaries commissions, are simply not helpful. Unsupported
allegations of "obviously mischievous" submissions have no place in the process.

If it wasn't for the majority's response to the minority report in the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries
Commission's Report, the Legislative Assembly of Alberta might have been able to give careful, thoughtful and
thorough consideration to the recommendations of the Commissioners, both the majority and the minority
Commissioners, without a lot of polarizing nonsense.

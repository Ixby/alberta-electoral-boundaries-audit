As an erstwhile lawyer and judge, numeracy is not something I ever possessed; but if one looks at the
last election, the UCP received roughly 52% of the popular vote and 49 seats or 57% of the seats in the
Legislature. The NDP received 44% of the popular vote and 38 seats or almost 44% of the seats. Not perfect,
but not bad.
Secondly, if you add up all the population variances in the electoral divisions mapped by the 2025-2026
Electoral Boundaries Commission, eliminate the Section 15(2) special consideration electoral divisions
(Central Peace-Notley, Lesser Slave Lake, and Canmore-Banff); and divide by the number of electoral
divisions, an average variance of 4.63% emerges, or 5.72% if you include the widely varying special
consideration electoral divisions. Pretty close to absolute parity, never mind relative population parity.
Kudos to the Commission, but minimal population variance tells you little about the merit of any particular
electoral division or electoral map. In Canada, a 4.6 % average variance is not bad. In the U.S., the courts
sometimes insist on less than a 1% variation. But look at the maps chasing the population parity produce.
It's not always gerrymandering that produces strange looking electoral divisions. Chasing population parity
can also produce them.

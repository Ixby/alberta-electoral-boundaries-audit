Minority Opinion - Panel of the Special Select Committee on Electoral Boundaries  29

111. The 1994 Reference held that there are three solutions to solve the problem of increased concentration of
     Alberta's population in urban centres:

         a) increasing the number of electoral divisions;
         b) moving rural electoral divisions to urban centres; and
         c) increasing the use of hybrids.

     The addition of two new electoral divisions means that the legislature has entirely solved the problem
     through "a)". Use of "c)" is accordingly unnecessary.

112. The population deviation caused by including Bearspaw and Springbank in Calgary is admittedly very small
     (about an additional 350 persons per Calgary electoral division). Coupled with their clear links to Calgary,
     it may seem persnickety for a court to strike down their inclusion, especially given the obiter dicta from
     Justice Miller. But it is nonetheless a real risk given the Panel's mandate and these unusual circumstances.

C. The Changes to Northeast and East Calgary Appear Unnecessary to Achieve Effective
     Representation

113. Very late in this Panel's mandate, the IAP Majority proposed that the Committee "consider" changes to
     four electoral divisions in Northeast and East Calgary: Calgary-Confluence, Calgary-Cross, Calgary-East,
     and Calgary-Klein. To repeat, we are of the view that this is beyond this Panel's mandate, being south of
     Airdrie and unconnected to the restoration of Lac Ste. Anne and Rocky Mountain House-Sundre. The
     IAP Majority writes that these changes may be advisable to keep the electoral divisions closer to the status
     quo and to reflect the locations of future population growth. However, the effect of these maps is to
     significantly increase the population disparity between these districts and between districts in Calgary
     overall. As we write elsewhere, high variations in populations between districts require compelling
     rationales based on improving effective representation. This is especially the case for districts within a
     single large urban municipality.

114. We find the rationale stated for these changes to fall short of such a compelling rationale. Our colleagues
     do not justify such a material departure from boundaries that create a more equal distribution of
     population as set out in the ABEBC Majority Report, and which is guaranteed to affect voter parity in the
     next election. The newly proposed boundaries for these four electoral divisions are also less rectangular
     and less clear than those in the ABEBC Majority Report, even if they are much more logical than the
     ABEBC Minority Report. Therefore, we do not support these proposed or suggested changes either. In
     any event, we are doubtful that they can be considered "necessary" to achieve effective representation.

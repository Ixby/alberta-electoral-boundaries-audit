# Rural variance usage — the minority's second charge, tested

**Status: exploratory** (same standard and same session as
`findings/rural_division_count.md`; hypotheses frozen in the script header
before first execution).

## The charge

> "That flexibility is not incidental; it is not a benign allowance for
> errors in mapmaking; it is a deliberate instruction. … To decline to use
> that range, particularly in rural Alberta, diminishes one of the Act's
> central safeguards." (minority report, p. 356)

The measurable claim: the majority's rural divisions sit close to the
provincial average — declining the Act's permitted below-average variance —
while the minority's make deliberate use of it.

## Results

Commission-published populations, rural/urban classification per the audit's
established name rule (corrected-variant figures in parentheses):

| Measure (rural-anchored divisions) | Majority 2026 | Minority 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Mean deviation from provincial average | **−6.0%** (−7.0%) | **−12.2%** (−13.7%) |
| Divisions below −10% | **4** (4) | **11** (11) |
| Divisions at s.15(2) scale (≤ −25%) | 3 (3) | 3 (3) |
| Most negative deviation | −47.7% | −45.4% |

## Verdict on the frozen hypotheses

**H-charge is supported.** The majority's rural divisions hug the provincial
mean; the minority's sit deep in the permitted negative range. On the
measure this charge names, the minority's accusation against the majority
holds — the majority drew rural Alberta at populations near parity rather
than using the Act's downward flexibility, and the minority did the
opposite.

## Reading the two charges together

The same page of the minority report makes two measurable accusations, and
they cut in opposite directions once tested:

- **Rural-division count** (`findings/rural_division_count.md`): reversed —
  the minority keeps fewer rural-anchored divisions than the majority
  (23 vs 28 against 2019's 31) and absorbs more of them into city-named
  hybrids.
- **Variance usage** (this file): supported — the rural divisions the
  minority does keep are drawn light (mean −12.2%), making real use of the
  Act's flexibility, where the majority's are drawn near parity (−6.0%).

Together they describe two coherent design philosophies rather than one
vice: the majority preserved *more* rural units at *fuller* populations;
the minority preserved *fewer* rural units at *lighter* populations and
attached the remainder to urban-anchored hybrids. Which approach better
serves "effective rural representation" is exactly the value dispute the
two reports had — the audit measures both sides of it and takes neither.

No intent is inferred in either direction, and neither result is a
population-equality verdict: the Act permits everything measured here.

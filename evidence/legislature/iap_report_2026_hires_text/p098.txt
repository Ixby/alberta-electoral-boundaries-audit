The fact that the Legislature expressly mandated that the advisory panel's proposals comply with the
population requirements of Section 15 and that they take into consideration effective representation and
the factors set forth in Section 14 of the Act, also indicates that the Legislature anticipated the possibility of
proposals being made for different electoral boundary configurations (different area, boundaries and names)
than those proposed by the Commission.

The minority argue that the advisory panel's review ought not to have resulted in proposals to change
what the 2025-2026 Electoral Boundaries Commission recommended except with respect to Lac Ste.
Anne-Parkland and Rimbey-Rocky Mountain House-Sundre. But the use of the phrase "make proposals
as to the area, boundaries, and names of the electoral divisions" certainly indicates that the Legislature
contemplated that proposals for change might be made. And the changes of course would be to the existing
electoral boundaries, not to electoral boundaries proposed by the Commission. The mandate was to review
and make proposals with respect to the electoral boundaries of Alberta, not the electoral boundaries
proposed by the Commission.

The advisory panel, in conducting its review, was also mandated to incorporate the 2025-2026 Electoral
Boundaries Commission Chairman's proposals in Recommendation 5 of the Chairman's Addendum to the
Majority Report, as noted in Clause C.(e)(ii)(B), "subject to any modifications the independent advisory panel
considers necessary, taking into consideration the requirement for effective representation as guaranteed by
the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms".

The Legislature, in Government Motion 37, acted on the Chairman's proposal that Section 13 of the
Electoral Boundaries Commission Act be amended to provide for 91 electoral divisions. It approved the
proposed increase. The Legislature also indicated approval of the Chairman's proposal to restore the two
electoral divisions which the Commission removed. As a consequence, the advisory panel was mandated
to incorporate that recommendation into its proposals, subject to any modification the panel considered
necessary. In other words, the Legislature approved of the restorations and mandated the panel to advise
how it might be accomplished.

There were other suggestions contained in Recommendation 5 although they were less clear. Some of the
suggestions were couched in mandatory terms. Some were couched in terms of requiring further "study".
The mandatory language employed by the Chairman in Recommendation 5 may not have been deliberate.
Clearly, it was inappropriate. Yet that mandatory language was seized upon by the minority of this panel as
prohibiting this panel from reviewing and making proposals with respect to electoral divisions other than
those directed by the Commission Chairman.

Electoral boundaries commissions make proposals. Electoral boundaries commissions do not direct,
mandate, restrict, prohibit, prescribe or stipulate anything. They also do not "impose" conditions on the
acceptance of their proposals. These are words and phrases used by the minority members of this advisory
panel to support their position. They were not words used by the Commission Chairman.

The Chairman's direction that "The rest of the province as we propose must be maintained to the extent
possible" would, if followed, render inoperative the Legislature's direction to the panel that it review all the
Province`s electoral boundaries and make proposals with respect to their area, boundaries and names. This
is an interpretation of the advisory panel's mandate which the Legislature's wording of Government Motion
37 clearly does not support.

The minority's argument that the advisory panel exceeded its mandate is also belied by what the panel has
actually done to discharge it. One need go no further than the Executive Summary to see that what the panel
has done is review the electoral boundary divisions of Alberta, make proposals with respect to the area and
boundaries of certain electoral divisions and incorporate the Commission Chairman's recommendations in
Recommendation 5. That was the panel's mandate.
